Football social media content ideas: use the quiet week to present your academy

By Martí Ruiz · 21 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • A week without a match is the best window to present your academy.
  • A simple question gives viewers a role and makes a Reel easy to answer and share.
  • Young players are new stories, and new stories build curiosity before they build opinion.
  • Plan quiet weeks in your calendar and compare results with your own median.
  • Always get written consent and check names before publishing youth content.

The most-watched Reel on the Spanish federation's profile this week did not show the senior team, and it did not show the Nations League draw. It showed a group of 17-year-olds at a World Cup. It reached 1.5 million views, about 2.3 times the profile median. For anyone collecting football social media content ideas, the message is bigger than one video: a week without a big match is not an empty week. It is the best window to present the players your audience does not know yet.

This post looks at what the federation did, why the pattern works, and how a club of any size can copy it with an academy, a women's team or a youth side.

What the daily analysis showed

The facts come from the daily analysis of @sefutbol on Instagram, with the draw information from UEFA.

  • On 20 November, Spain learned its Nations League quarter-final opponent: France.
  • The federation published the draw with a question: how are we going to wait until March? The post collected 88,000 likes and 776 comments.
  • That is a strong result. But the profile's Reels tab told another story.
  • The Reel that was most watched this week was about the under-17 team at their World Cup, built around a simple question: what name will you put on your shirt?
  • It reached 1.5 million views, more than double the usual level of the profile.

Football social media content ideas: the quiet week is your academy window

The draw was the news. The under-17 Reel was the content. Both can live on the same profile, and they do different jobs. The draw serves the fans who already follow the senior team. The youth Reel introduces something new. Most fans do not know those faces yet, so each player is a small discovery. That is why it works: you are not repeating a story, you are starting one.

A new face is a new story

When you show a famous player, the audience arrives with an opinion. When you show a young player, the audience arrives with curiosity. Curiosity is easier to turn into a follow, a save or a share. It also gives your club something to build on for years, because today's under-17 is tomorrow's debutant.

A simple question gives the viewer a role

The question about the name on the shirt is easy to answer. It does not require football knowledge. It invites the viewer to imagine the player as a person with a name, a family and a number. A question like this is a very good fit for sports reels ideas, because it works in the first seconds and keeps working if the sound is off.

A quiet week lowers the competition

On a match week, your academy content competes with the first team inside your own feed. In a week without a game, it becomes the headline. You also have time to film properly, to ask the player a question and to edit with care. That is a core point for any sports social media strategy: decide what each part of the calendar is for.

Ten ideas to present your academy in a quiet week

You do not need a World Cup. A regional tournament, a training camp or a school visit is enough. These sports content ideas work for a first-division club and for a small amateur side. Use them as sports social media content ideas you can plan weeks ahead.

  1. The name on the shirt. Ask each young player what name they would put on the back. Short answers, one face per clip.
  2. First-team shirt, academy player. Let a young player try the senior kit and react. Keep it natural and unscripted.
  3. The first boots. Ask players to show the oldest pair of boots they own and tell where they got them.
  4. One day, one player. Follow a single academy player from school to training. A simple day in the life, without extra production.
  5. The coach's three words. A youth coach describes each player in three words. Fans love short labels.
  6. Who is the fastest, the funniest, the loudest. A dressing-room vote with a name on each answer. Easy to repeat every month.
  7. Meet the family. A parent or sibling explains how the player got started. Check permissions, especially for minors.
  8. The first-team player remembers. A senior player who came through the academy visits and answers questions from the kids.
  9. Number card carousel. One slide per player: name, position, one fun fact. A carousel that fans can save as a reference.
  10. Ask the fans to pick. Which young player should we follow next? Let comments guide the next piece.

How to split it by platform

The same content can be cut in different ways. Here is a simple split.

  • Instagram: the question Reel as the main piece, the number cards as a carousel, quick answers in Stories. These are the core sports Instagram content ideas for the academy.
  • TikTok: shorter, more informal, with the most surprising answer in the first frame. For sports TikTok content ideas, let the players talk like themselves and avoid heavy graphics.
  • Main account vs. academy account: if the academy has its own profile, post there first and bring the best piece to the main account. The federation example shows the main account can carry youth content and benefit from it.

Remember the safety rules

Youth content needs care. Ask for written consent from parents or guardians before publishing. Avoid showing home addresses, schools or anything that identifies private details. Agree with the club and the federation which content is allowed. When in doubt, ask the player and the family before posting.

How to plan quiet weeks before they arrive

Quiet weeks are predictable. International breaks, off-season pauses and postponed fixtures are known long before they happen. That makes them easy to plan, and it means you do not have to improvise content at the last minute.

Mark them in the calendar

Add every quiet week to your sports content calendar and give each one a theme: academy, archive, women's team, fan stories. Then you know what to film in the busy weeks, so that the quiet ones are already half done.

Assign roles

A sports content planning tool helps you decide who films, who edits, who asks permission and who publishes each piece. Academy content often depends on one person who has access to the youth coaches. Share that access across the team.

Speed up the edit

Many sports content creation tools can trim, caption and resize a clip for several platforms in minutes. AI tools for sports content creation can suggest subtitles or a first draft of a caption, but a person should always check names, spelling and tone. With young players, the spelling of a name matters a lot.

Compare with your own median

The under-17 Reel stood out because it was compared with the profile's usual results: about 2.3 times the median. Do the same. Then use your sports marketing tools to put that ratio in a short report for the commercial team and your partners. A clear ratio is easier to present than a raw number, and it shows that academy content has real value.

Takeaway

The pattern is simple. Use the news for the people who already follow you, and use the quiet week to introduce the people they have not met yet. A good question, a clear face and a small story are enough. These social media ideas for sports teams do not need a big budget, only a plan. They also follow one of the most useful sports social media trends of the moment: the accounts that stand out keep publishing something worth watching even when there is no match. Before your next break, ask yourself one question: when did your academy last appear on your main account?

Frequently asked questions

Why did the under-17 Reel outperform the senior team content?

It introduced new faces with a simple question, so viewers could take part without football knowledge. It reached about 2.3 times the profile median.

Can small clubs copy this idea?

Yes. You only need a few academy players, a simple question and parental consent. A phone and a quiet training session are enough.

Should academy content go on the main account or a separate one?

Both can work. Post first where the youth audience is, then bring the best piece to the main account to reach more fans.

How do I plan content for weeks without matches?

Mark international breaks and pauses in your content calendar, give each one a theme, and film the material in busier weeks so you are ready.
