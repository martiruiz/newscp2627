How Atlético de Madrid turned a gameday graphic into a high-impact sponsorship asset

By Josep · 25 March 2026 · 4 min read

Most teams post a gameday graphic. Few turn it into a strategic asset.

In social media in sports, these posts consistently deliver strong reach and engagement. But too often, they’re treated as routine content instead of premium digital inventory.

Atlético de Madrid recently showed how small decisions in content structure and activation can elevate a simple post into a powerful sports marketing tool.

The takeaway is clear: it’s not about creating more content, but about structuring it better.

The Atlético de Madrid x Netflix case: more than a gameday graphic

Atlético de Madrid partnered with Netflix for a Peaky Blinders-themed takeover during a matchday.

At first glance, it might look like a standard collaboration.

But the execution tells a different story.

The gameday graphic wasn’t just branded – it was integrated into a broader narrative:

  • Visual identity aligned with the Peaky Blinders aesthetic

  • Consistent storytelling across multiple touchpoints

  • Clear connection between the matchday moment and the sponsor

This is where most teams fall short.

They focus on placing a logo. Atlético focused on creating an experience.

For any content creator, this is the difference between content that fills a calendar and content that drives real fan engagement.

What Atlético did right (and why it matters)

Contextual integration

The activation wasn’t forced.

Peaky Blinders has a strong, recognizable aesthetic. Atlético adapted its gameday graphic to reflect that identity, making the content feel native rather than sponsored.

Impact:

  • Higher engagement

  • Stronger brand recall

  • More shareable content

Strategic timing

The collaboration happened on matchday – the peak moment of attention.


Instead of relying on secondary formats (like Instagram Stories), the activation was placed in a high-performing asset.


Impact:

  • Maximum visibility

  • Guaranteed reach baseline

  • Better ROI for the partner


Content ecosystem thinking

The gameday graphic was not an isolated post. It was part of a wider activation.

This is key in modern digital content:

  • Pre-match hype

  • Matchday visuals

  • Cross-platform storytelling


Impact:

  • Deeper fan immersion

  • Extended campaign lifespan

  • More touchpoints for the sponsor

The hidden strategy: flexible inventory wins

Here’s what’s interesting.

For Atlético to execute this kind of activation, they needed flexibility in their content inventory.

If the gameday graphic had been locked into a rigid, season-long sponsorship deal, this collaboration becomes harder – or even impossible.

This is where many clubs lose opportunities.

By not structuring their assets properly, they limit:

  • Short-term partnerships

Turning this into a replicable framework

You don’t need a Netflix partnership to apply this.

Here’s how clubs, agencies and content creators can replicate the approach:

1. Identify your premium moments

Not all posts are equal.


Your gameday graphic is:

  • High reach

  • High engagement

  • Highly predictable


Treat it as premium inventory.


2. Keep strategic flexibility

Avoid locking all gameday graphics into one package.

Instead:

  • Reserve key matchdays (home games, derbies, theme nights)

  • Keep slots open for short-term activations


This allows you to react quickly to opportunities.

3. Think beyond logo placement

Ask:

  • Can the sponsor influence the visual identity?

  • Can the content reflect the brand’s universe?


Atlético didn’t add Netflix. They adapted the content to fit Peaky Blinders.


That’s the shift.

4. Build content ecosystems

Don’t isolate the gameday graphic.

Connect it with:

  • Teaser content

  • Behind-the-scenes

  • Matchday storytelling


This multiplies the value of each activation.

5. Align creative and commercial teams

This is where many organizations fail.

Content and sales need to work together:

  • Define flexible inventory in advance

  • Plan potential activation scenarios

  • Share performance data


Tools like Sports Content Playbook can help here.


Instead of starting from scratch, teams can:

  • Access proven activation ideas

  • Structure content calendars strategically

  • Identify which moments are best for partnerships


For example, you could map out high-profile home games as “activation-ready” slots and prepare adaptable creative concepts in advance.

Why this matters for social media in sports

The Atlético de Madrid case highlights a broader shift. In social media in sports, value is no longer just about reach.

It’s about:

  • Context

  • Creativity

  • Integration


Fans don’t engage with ads. They engage with experiences. And brands don’t just want visibility. They want relevance.

For any content creator working in sports, this changes the game. You’re not just producing posts.

You’re:

  • Designing digital products

  • Supporting revenue generation

  • Building brand narratives


The gameday graphic is just the starting point.

The risk of not adapting

If you keep treating gameday graphics as static assets, you’ll face:

  • Lower perceived value from sponsors

  • Missed short-term opportunities

  • Generic content that blends in

Meanwhile, clubs that adapt – like Atlético – will:

  • Command higher sponsorship value

  • Deliver better fan engagement

  • Build stronger brand positioning

Small changes in structure can create massive competitive advantages.

Tip of the week

Before your next big home game, design a themed version of your gameday graphic aligned with a potential sponsor category – even if you don’t have a partner yet.
