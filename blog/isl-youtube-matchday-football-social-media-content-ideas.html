One match, many feeds: football social media content ideas from the ISL's YouTube start

By Martí Ruiz · 16 October 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • The ISL drew 65 million YouTube views across five matches in the opening week of its 2026-27 season, adding 120,000 subscribers to the league's channel.
  • Every ISL match is streamed three times at once: an English feed, a Hindi feed and a vertical feed for phones, and all three stay on the channel as replays.
  • Regional-language versions can outperform the main one: the Bengali highlights of NorthEast United vs Mohun Bagan had almost twice the views of the English highlights.
  • The best-performing ISL Shorts were not goals but emotional moments, such as an 8-second near-miss and a crowd reacting to a stoppage-time winner.
  • Recurring series like #TeamTalk and #ISLThrowback fill the days between matches and give every club a regular slot in the content calendar.

The Indian Super League has started its 2026-27 season with a number that every league and club media team should look at twice: 65 million YouTube views in the opening week, across just five matches. According to Business Standard, those games reached 52 million viewers, generated 16.8 million engaged views and 1.6 million live interactions in chat, and added 120,000 subscribers to the league's channel, which now has 2.7 million. The interesting part is not the total. It is how the league builds it: every match becomes a whole family of videos, each made for a different screen, language and moment. That system is a goldmine of football social media content ideas for anyone who owns live rights, or just access.

Every ISL match is live and free on YouTube, alongside TV coverage on Sony Sports Network and streaming on SonyLIV. The season runs from 10 October to 31 May 2027, so this is not a launch stunt. It is a format the league will repeat every week for eight months.

One match, three live feeds

Open the league's live tab and the pattern is obvious. Each fixture is streamed three times at once: an English feed, a Hindi feed and a vertical feed built for people watching on a phone held upright. After the final whistle, all three stay on the channel as full replays.

The biggest fixture of the week shows how much the main feed can carry. NorthEast United FC's 1-0 win over Mohun Bagan Super Giant recorded 17 million views on the English feed, the most-viewed match of the week. Its full replay showed close to 18 million views when we checked on 16 October.

The other feeds look small next to it, but they are not. The Hindi feed of the same game peaked at 100,660 concurrent viewers, and its replay had over 930,000 views on 16 October. The vertical replay had more than 500,000. Those are audiences the league would not have reached with a single horizontal stream. The vertical feed is the clearest signal of where viewing is going: a full 90 minutes framed for the same screen where fans already watch Shorts and Reels.

You do not need broadcast rights to borrow this idea. A club can run a vertical watch-along, a live pre-match show from the tunnel or a post-match press conference streamed upright. The principle is the same: pick the screen first, then frame the content for it. This is one of the clearest sports social media trends of the season, and it is not limited to YouTube.

Speak the fans' language, literally

The second layer is language. The ISL publishes highlights in English and in regional languages, and the numbers show why. For NorthEast United's late winner against Mohun Bagan, the Bengali highlights package had 64,607 views on 16 October, almost twice the 34,106 of the English highlights of the same match.

Mohun Bagan is a Kolkata club with a huge Bengali-speaking fan base, so the result makes sense. But most clubs never test it. If a meaningful part of your audience speaks another language, a second version of your best video is one of the cheapest growth levers you have. Subtitles, a local commentator or a native-speaking presenter can be enough. Today, many sports content creation tools and AI tools for sports content creation can draft translations and subtitles in minutes, which leaves the media team free to check the tone and the club vocabulary.

Shorts that sell the feeling, not the result

The third layer is short vertical clips, and here the ISL does something smart: the best-performing Shorts of the week are not goals. The top one, with over 180,000 views, is an 8-second near-miss: Churchill Brothers almost scoring a second goal at the Fatorda.

Right behind it is a 12-second clip of the crowd reacting to Jithin's stoppage-time winner for NorthEast United, “blowing the roof off” the stadium in Guwahati. It had more than 155,000 views, over four times the English highlights of the same match.

A third, “It's for the badge, it's for the people!”, is pure sound and emotion in 10 seconds, and passed 86,000 views. Near-misses, noise and pride: moments that make you feel the match, not just know the score. These are the sports reels ideas that travel, because they work for someone who did not watch the game.

Series that fill the days between matches

A league plays a few days a week, but the channel posts every day. The ISL fills the gaps with recurring series: #TeamTalk, a short episode per club about its summer changes and squad, quick highlights for people with two minutes, #ISLThrowback matches from last season, and personality clips such as player blind rankings. Each series is a slot in the sports content calendar that the team does not have to reinvent every week.

The #TeamTalk episodes do not get the views of a near-miss, and they do not need to. They give each of the league's clubs a moment in the spotlight, give fans of smaller teams a reason to subscribe and give partners a steady place to appear. In a solid sports social media strategy, series are the backbone and viral clips are the bonus.

The pattern behind the numbers: Football social media content ideas that scale

Put the layers together and you get a model that any league, club or federation can adapt to its own size:

  • The live match is the anchor. Everything else is cut from it, so one event feeds a full week of posts.
  • Each screen gets its own version. Horizontal for TV and desktop, vertical for phones, 4:5 for feeds.
  • Each audience gets its own language. Regional versions can outperform the main one.
  • Short clips sell emotion. Near-misses, crowd noise and pride beat a plain goal replay.
  • Series keep the channel alive between matches. They make planning predictable and give every club a turn.

None of this depends on YouTube alone. The same clips work as sports Instagram content ideas on Reels and as sports TikTok content ideas, with the caption and the first second adapted to each platform.

Social media ideas for sports teams to try this month

Here are practical sports content ideas and social media ideas for sports teams based on the ISL model, scaled for clubs with smaller crews:

  1. Run a vertical watch-along. If you cannot stream the match, stream the reactions: a presenter, two fans and a phone held upright on matchday.
  2. Make a second-language version of your top video each week. Start with your largest non-native audience and compare the results after a month.
  3. Film the crowd at the big moments. Keep one camera on the stands for late goals and near-misses; the reaction often outperforms the action.
  4. Post the near-miss. The chance that almost went in is drama without spoiling the result, ideal for sports social media content ideas the day after.
  5. Launch a weekly series. A one-minute “team talk”, a squad blind ranking or a throwback match gives you a fixed slot in the calendar.
  6. Turn one match into five formats. Full replay, quick highlights, a crowd Short, a near-miss Short and a 4:5 feed clip.
  7. Repeat the formula every week. The ISL's value comes from consistency over an eight-month season, not from one viral week.

Takeaway

The ISL's opening week is a lesson in multiplication, not in budget. One match becomes three live feeds, several highlight packages in different languages, and a stream of short emotional clips, framed for every screen and audience. Most sports marketing tools and sports social media tools can help you schedule and measure that work, but the idea comes first: decide who you are talking to, on which screen, in which language, and make a version for each. Frequently asked questions

How did the ISL get 65 million YouTube views in one week?

The ISL streams every match live and free on YouTube and turns each one into a family of videos. Each fixture gets English, Hindi and vertical live feeds, regional-language highlights, short emotional clips and recurring series between matches.

What football social media content ideas can clubs copy from the ISL?

Clubs can run a vertical watch-along, make a second-language version of their top video each week and film the crowd at big moments. They can also post near-misses, launch a weekly series and turn one match into five formats: full replay, quick highlights, a crowd Short, a near-miss Short and a 4:5 feed clip.

Do football clubs need broadcast rights to use vertical live streams?

No. A club without rights can stream a vertical watch-along with a presenter and fans, a live pre-match show from the tunnel or a post-match press conference filmed upright. The principle is to pick the screen first and frame the content for it.

What kind of football Shorts perform best?
