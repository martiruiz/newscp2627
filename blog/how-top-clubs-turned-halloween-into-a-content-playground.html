Sports content ideas

How top clubs turned Halloween into a content playground

By Josep · 3 November 2025 · 4 min read

Halloween has become one of the most creative moments on the sports content calendar. For clubs and franchises, it’s not just a festive post — it’s a yearly stress test of originality, brand tone, and audience engagement. The best activations go beyond costumes and filters: they build community participation, reveal player personality, and turn a cultural event into a brand storytelling opportunity.

This week, we break down how five professional teams — Borussia Dortmund, Burnley FC, Club Brugge, Inter Milan, and the Milwaukee Bucks — each found their own way to own Halloween on social media. From interactive reels to digital illustrations, each post showcases how creativity and authenticity continue to redefine fan connection in 2025.

Borussia Dortmund – Turning Halloween Into an Interactive Challenge

Borussia Dortmund’s Halloween post transformed a simple video into an interactive fan game. The reel invited followers to “pause” the video at just the right moment to place a mask on one of the club’s players — a lighthearted challenge that encouraged fans to engage directly with the content.

It was a clever use of the pause mechanic, a format trend that’s been resurfacing on Reels and TikTok. By gamifying a simple action, Dortmund created a sense of play and community without complex production. The payoff? High engagement in comments, fans sharing screenshots of their “mask moment,” and a post that felt both human and innovative.

The activation worked because it made participation effortless and fun.

Burnley FC – Scares, Smiles, and Team Chemistry

Burnley’s Halloween reel leaned fully into humor and authenticity. In a collaborative post format, the English club produced a compilation of players being startled by classic Halloween scares. Some jumped, others barely reacted — and that mix became the content’s charm.

Rather than relying on slick production or heavy effects, Burnley captured raw, relatable reactions that made supporters laugh and comment. The tone was intentionally casual — the kind of video that feels like it could have been shot on a phone, but still delivers a professional edit.

It’s an excellent example of how small-scale, personality-led videos can outperform expensive campaigns when the emotional connection is real.

Club Brugge – The surprise box that never fails

Club Brugge opted for a classic prank setup — the “mystery box” experiment. Players were told to open a box expecting a surprise or gift, only to be met with a fake spider jumping out at them. The reactions ranged from startled screams to knowing smiles, giving the reel a perfect rhythm of tension and release.

While the concept isn’t new, Brugge’s execution proves why it still works: tight pacing, expressive player reactions, and an editing style that amplifies the suspense. Fans love seeing players out of their comfort zones, and the video perfectly captures that human side of the squad.

Familiar format, reliable results — and proof that emotional reactions remain some of the most shareable assets in sports content.

Inter Milan – Horror films and connection with the fans

Inter Milan took a different approach — one rooted in conversation rather than pranks. During a training session, the club’s media team asked players to share their favorite horror movies, capturing candid, unscripted answers on camera.

This format plays directly into Inter’s modern communication tone: elegant, human, and quietly playful. It builds proximity between players and fans by connecting through everyday culture, not just football. Each answer reveals personality, humor, and a bit of vulnerability — traits that deepen fan affinity.

By using a simple question format, Inter turned Halloween into a moment of genuine storytelling. It’s proof that content doesn’t always need a big idea — sometimes, curiosity is enough.

Milwaukee Bucks – Art direction meets festivity

The Milwaukee Bucks delivered a standout Halloween post rooted in visual design rather than video. The NBA franchise published a carousel featuring a digital illustration of the entire roster dressed in Halloween costumes. The first slide wished fans a “Happy Halloween” in the Bucks’ signature typography and color palette, while the following slides revealed individual player caricatures — each stylized, fun, and instantly recognizable.

This content type bridges the gap between sport and pop art. It reinforces the Bucks’ identity as a design-forward franchise and provides fans with saveable, shareable visuals. By going the illustration route, the Bucks avoided the crowded “costume video” lane and instead owned a creative niche where design is the hero.

It’s a reminder that not every club needs to produce physical activations; sometimes, good art direction is enough to win the scroll.

Closing thoughts

This year’s Halloween content shows how clubs are becoming more strategic and creative with their seasonal storytelling. From interactive Reels to digital illustrations, the best teams understand that seasonal moments are not just about the holiday itself — they’re a sandbox for testing tone, format, and fan engagement in real time.

For communicators and social teams, the takeaway is simple:

  • Use cultural moments to reveal personality, not just promote
  • Design activations that encourage participation, laughter, or recognition.
  • Stay true to your brand voice — even when it’s wearing a costume.

Halloween may last one night, but the creative lessons from these campaigns can inspire content planning all year long.
