Women’s football on social media: why English and Spanish clubs play different games

By Josep · 19 October 2026 · 3 min read

Women’s football is growing, but not every club is developing its digital presence at the same speed.

While some organizations invest in creative videos, player storytelling, and brand collaborations, others rely more heavily on traditional photography and matchday graphics.

Comparing London City Lionesses, FC Badalona Women, Tottenham Hotspur, and FC Barcelona Femení reveals how resources, club structure, and content strategy influence social media in sports.

London City vs. Badalona Women: two different digital realities

Both London City Lionesses and FC Badalona Women operate independently from major men’s football clubs, but their Instagram strategies differ significantly.

London City: creativity and player-focused content

With approximately 668,000 Instagram followers in the analyzed snapshot, London City has developed several recognizable content pillars:

  • Graphics celebrating international call-ups.

  • Reels featuring goals and highlights.

  • Creative videos welcoming international players back.

  • Challenges and entertaining content between teammates.

  • Six pinned posts showcasing the club’s identity.

Its strategy combines information, entertainment, and player personalities, creating multiple opportunities for fan engagement.

Badalona Women: fewer resources, valuable opportunities

FC Badalona Women has approximately 22,500 followers and relies more heavily on photography, with fewer Reels and experimental formats.

Although this may reflect differences in production resources, the club has a valuable storytelling asset: Vicky Losada.

The former Barcelona player could become central to recurring videos, training challenges, and behind-the-scenes content.

The lesson is simple: smaller clubs do not necessarily need bigger budgets. They need recognizable formats and authentic personalities.

Tottenham vs. Barcelona: the power of collaborations

Tottenham Women and FC Barcelona Femení benefit from established football institutions, but their digital reach differs considerably.

Tottenham: collaboration increases visibility

With more than half a million followers, Tottenham Women generates noticeably greater engagement when publications are shared collaboratively with the main club account.

These collaborations introduce women’s football to broader audiences.

However, relying too heavily on them creates a challenge: converting temporary visibility into independent engagement.

Barcelona: a powerful digital ecosystem

With approximately 7.2 million Instagram followers, FC Barcelona Femení operates on a much larger scale.

Its strategy combines sporting content, entertainment, player personalities, and commercial partnerships.

Recent collaborations involving Google Pixel and content connected to the men’s Clásico demonstrate how the women’s team participates in Barcelona’s wider marketing ecosystem.

Even simple videos featuring players such as Cata Coll can achieve visibility comparable to collaborative posts from smaller clubs.

However, comparing engagement rates rather than absolute interactions provides a fairer assessment.

What these strategies teach us about social media in sports

Three factors explain many of these differences.

Investment creates opportunities. Larger communication teams can produce more video content and experiment with formats.

Players build emotional connections. Recognizable personalities help clubs attract audiences beyond traditional supporters.

Collaborations expand reach. However, consistent original content is essential for developing long-term loyalty.

For any content creator, the challenge is combining these elements without depending exclusively on budget or established brand recognition.

A practical framework for women’s football clubs

Clubs can improve their digital strategies through four actions:

  1. Analyze content performance: compare photography, Reels, entertainment, and matchday coverage.

  2. Create recurring formats: introduce weekly player challenges or behind-the-scenes videos.

  3. Use collaborations strategically: connect with larger accounts during important announcements.

  4. Measure meaningful results: prioritize engagement rates, shares, saves, and follower growth over total followers.

These actions can help smaller organizations build stronger communities while allowing established clubs to improve their content efficiency.
