Social media in sports: why the best sponsored content creates the moment instead of interrupting it

By Josep · 12 June 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • The Philadelphia Flyers' Look-A-Like Cam video from The Office Game presented by Peacock generated the highest social value of any NHL team post this season.
  • The post reached 5.5 million views, 677,300 engagements, more than 100,000 shares and $122,289 in social value, 17% more than the second-best NHL post.
  • Fans do not reject sponsored content; they reject content that gives them no reason to care.
  • The strongest sponsorships create experiences that would not exist otherwise and put fan value first, with the sponsor message second.
  • Content teams should be involved before sponsorship packages are finalized, so activations are designed for online audiences and not only for people in the venue.

One of the biggest misconceptions in sports marketing and social media in sports is that sponsored content is destined to underperform.

Many teams, leagues, and brands still assume that adding a sponsor automatically makes content less engaging. The data suggests otherwise. One of the most valuable Instagram posts across all NHL teams this season was not a game-winning goal, a championship celebration, or a viral player moment.

It was sponsored content. The Philadelphia Flyers generated the highest social value in the league with a video from their The Office-themed Look-A-Like Cam during The Office Game presented by Peacock.

The results were remarkable:

  • 5.5 million views
  • 677,300 engagements
  • More than 100,000 shares
  • 12.3% engagement rate
  • $122,289 in social value


Most importantly, the activation generated 17% more social value than the second-best post in the NHL. This case highlights an important truth for anyone working in social media in sports: fans do not reject sponsored content. They reject content that gives them no reason to care.

The activation created the content

The most important lesson from the Flyers example is that the sponsor was not attached to an existing moment. The sponsor created the moment. Without Peacock:

  • There is no The Office Game.
  • There is no Look-A-Like Cam.
  • There is no viral content.

That distinction changes everything. Too often, sponsorship is treated as an interruption layered onto content. The most successful partnerships work differently. They create experiences that would not exist otherwise. Fans engage because the activation is entertaining, not because it is sponsored. The sponsor simply becomes part of the experience.

Why the activation worked

When analyzing successful digital content, the same principles appear repeatedly. Whether content is sponsored or organic, audiences are generally looking for four things:

Entertainment

Can it make me laugh or smile?

Emotional connection

Can it make me feel something?

Education

Can it teach me something useful?

Utility

Can it help me accomplish something?

The Flyers activation delivered two of the strongest drivers. It was entertaining. And it leveraged nostalgia through one of the most recognizable television series in modern culture. The audience did not share the post because Peacock sponsored it. They shared it because the content was fun. This is a critical lesson for every content creator building sponsorship campaigns. The value proposition must always be fan-first.

The biggest mistake teams still make

Many organizations design activations backwards. The process often looks like this:

  1. A marketing message is defined.

The fan becomes an afterthought. When that happens:

  • Engagement decreases.
  • Sponsors receive weaker results.
  • Content teams lose efficiency.
  • Future sponsorship sales become more difficult.

The strongest partnerships start with audience value. The sponsor message comes second.

Content teams should be involved from day one

One of the most overlooked insights from successful sponsorship programs is the role of content teams. Too often, sponsorship departments sell an activation before social media teams are involved.

By then, the concept is already locked. This creates problems because many in-venue activations are designed for spectators inside the venue, not audiences consuming content online. For example:

  • Visit our booth.
  • Scan this QR code.
  • Enter our contest.

These tactics may work in person. They rarely perform on social media. Content teams need a seat at the table before sponsorship packages are finalized. Their job is not simply to film the activation. Their job is to help design something people actually want to watch.

A framework for creating better sponsored content in social media in sports

The Flyers example can be transformed into a repeatable framework.

1. Start with entertainment

Ask yourself: Would fans enjoy this if the sponsor disappeared?

If the answer is no, rethink the concept.

2. Build experiences, not advertisements

The best sponsorships create memories. Examples include:

  • Look-A-Like Cams
  • Fan races
  • Celebrity interactions
  • Interactive challenges
  • Unique halftime experiences

3. Keep integrations simple

One common mistake is over-branding. In most cases:

  • Logo placement is enough.
  • A simple tag is enough.
  • A subtle “presented by” mention is enough.

Fans should remember the experience first.

4. Create multiple content angles

One activation can generate:

  • Fan reactions
  • Behind-the-scenes content
  • Creator collaborations
  • Short-form highlights
  • Sponsor recaps

The activation creates the moment. The content extends its lifespan.

The hidden value of sponsored experiences

One reason organizations underestimate sponsorship content is because they evaluate it too narrowly.

A single social post can become:

  • Email content
  • Paid advertising creative
  • Website content
  • Mobile app content
  • Creator collaboration material
  • Sponsor-owned content

The same activation can serve multiple business objectives simultaneously. That is why sponsorship content should never be considered bonus inventory. It is premium inventory. The Flyers example demonstrates how a single activation can generate significant awareness, engagement, and measurable value for every stakeholder involved.

Why this matters for social media in sports

Sports organizations are competing for attention in increasingly crowded feeds. The teams that succeed are not necessarily those with the largest audiences. They are the teams creating experiences people want to share. The future of sports sponsorship is not about inserting logos into content.

It is about designing moments worth talking about. When sponsorship becomes part of the experience rather than an interruption, everybody wins. Fans get memorable moments. Teams get stronger content. Sponsors get meaningful exposure.

Frequently asked questions

Does sponsored content perform worse on social media in sports?

Not necessarily. The Philadelphia Flyers' sponsored Look-A-Like Cam video was one of the most valuable Instagram posts across all NHL teams this season. Sponsored content performs well when the activation gives fans a genuine reason to care.

Why did the Flyers' Look-A-Like Cam activation work so well?

It was entertaining and tapped into nostalgia through The Office, one of the most recognizable TV series in modern culture. Fans shared it because it was fun, not because Peacock sponsored it. The sponsor created the moment instead of interrupting it.

What is the biggest mistake teams make with sponsored activations?

Many organizations design activations backwards: they define a marketing message first, build an activation around it, and then ask social media to amplify it. The fan becomes an afterthought, which lowers engagement and weakens results for sponsors.

How can teams create better sponsored content?

Start with entertainment and ask whether fans would enjoy it if the sponsor disappeared. Build experiences rather than advertisements, keep brand integrations simple, and plan multiple content angles from one activation. Involving content teams from day one also helps design something people actually want to watch.
