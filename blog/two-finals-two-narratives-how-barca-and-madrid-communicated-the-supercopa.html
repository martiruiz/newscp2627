Two finals, two narratives: How Barça and Madrid communicated the Supercopa

By Josep · 13 January 2026 · 1 min read

The Spanish Super Cup final did not only deliver an intense 3–2 scoreline, but also offered a clear case study in social media in sports. The way FC Barcelona and Real Madrid approached post-match communication showed how results on the pitch directly influence editorial ambition, format choice, and storytelling volume – an insight especially relevant for any content creator working in elite football.

As champions, FC Barcelona activated a celebratory, video-first strategy designed to maximize emotion and shareability. Rather than relying solely on trophy visuals, Barça leaned into authentic moments that felt human and spontaneous. A locker-room video featuring Lamine Yamal, Alejandro Balde, and Roony Bardghji dancing with their medals became one of the most engaging pieces, capturing youth, chemistry, and joy.

Another standout asset used symbolism and design: a blaugrana silhouette fading into the outline of the Supercopa trophy. These formats went beyond reporting – they created narrative and mood, driving strong engagement across social media in sports platforms.

Real Madrid, following defeat, chose restraint. Their communication was minimal and controlled, limited to a tunnel-walk video before kick-off and two static score updates at half-time and full-time. Each result post featured the goalscorers, Vinícius Jr. and Gonzalo García, without additional emotional framing. This low-volume approach prioritized brand consistency and discretion over amplification – another valid editorial choice.

For any content creator, this contrast highlights how victory often enables creativity, while defeat encourages control. At SCP, and across SCP’s curated examples, these moments show that strategy matters as much as outcome.
