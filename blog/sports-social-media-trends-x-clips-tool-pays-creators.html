Sports social media trends: X now pays for clips, and long video becomes an asset

By Martí Ruiz · 13 November 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • X's clips tool lets the clipper and the owner of the original video earn from views.
  • Each clip links back to the source, so clubs can be the origin of their own moments.
  • Long video such as press conferences and matches is a quarry of clips.
  • Clip length limits and revenue split are not confirmed, so check them before planning.
  • Prepare a calendar, an owner and a simple cutting routine now.

X has just launched its clips tool, and it changes how a long video can be used. Among the latest sports social media trends, this is one of the most practical for clubs, leagues, federations, athletes and brands. The news comes from TechCrunch and shows a simple idea: a clip is no longer a copy. It is distribution.

What X has launched

According to the material from the daily analysis, the tool works in a few steps:

  • You take a video.
  • You trim the best moment.
  • You add your own opinion.
  • You publish it without leaving the app.

Each clip carries a label that links back to the original video. The key point is the money. The person who trims the clip and the person who created the video can both earn from the views.

Two details are not confirmed in the material: the maximum length of a clip and how revenue is shared. Before you plan around the tool, check both in the official X information.

Why it matters for sports teams

Sport produces long video all the time. A press conference of twenty minutes has ten clips inside. A match has a hundred. Until now, many of those moments were cut and shared by fans without the club getting the credit or the benefit. With a label that links to the original, the club can be the source of the clip instead of a bystander.

This also fits what we see in profiles that stand out. They do not treat each video as a one-off. They own a long piece, cut it into many short ones and place each on the platform where it works best. The clip is the unit, and the long video is the quarry.

The pattern to replicate

The accounts with the strongest content habits tend to share the same routine. This is a good base for your sports social media strategy:

  1. Record more than you publish. Press conferences, training, tunnel and bench footage are raw material.
  2. Mark the moments. Note the minute of each good line, reaction or play while you record.
  3. Cut for each platform. The same moment can be a clip on X, a Reel on Instagram and a vertical video on TikTok.
  4. Add a point of view. The new tool invites an opinion on top of the clip. A short line from the club voice turns a cut into content.
  5. Link back to the source. The original video keeps the full context and the audience you want to bring in.

Sports social media content ideas you can use this week

These sports social media content ideas work with long video you already have. Think of them as simple sports content ideas that any team can start with, whatever its size:

  • The best answer of a coach in the press conference, with one line of context.
  • The first reaction of a player after a goal or a win, cut to under a minute.
  • A tactical explanation from the analyst, one play at a time.
  • A legend's story from an old interview, clipped for an anniversary.
  • A fan moment from the stands taken from the match broadcast, if you have the rights.

For football social media content ideas, think of the manager's pre-match answer, a captain's speech or a substitute's celebration. The same cuts feed sports Instagram content ideas and sports TikTok content ideas, and they are a clear base for sports reels ideas. The question is no longer whether fans will clip your video. It is whether you will do it first, with your voice and a link to the source. Count the clips in your last press conference, and start there.

Frequently asked questions

What is the X clips tool?

It lets users trim the best moment of a video, add an opinion and publish it in the app. Each clip is labelled with a link to the original video.

Who can earn money from the clips?

According to the material, both the person who trims the clip and the creator of the original video can earn from the views.

Is there a clip length limit or a fixed revenue split?

This could not be verified in the material. Check the official X information before building a plan.

How can a sports team prepare?

Record more long video, mark the best moments, assign who cuts and approves, and add a sharp line of context to each clip.
