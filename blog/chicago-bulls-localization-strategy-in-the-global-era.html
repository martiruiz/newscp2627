Chicago Bulls: Localization strategy in the global era

By Josep · 27 November 2025 · 2 min read

In a world where basketball is global — not just American — the Chicago Bulls have taken a deliberate step beyond translation: they’ve launched a fully localized social presence to connect with Spanish-speaking fans. This move isn’t an afterthought — it’s a strategic bid to grow fandom, deepen brand relevance, and unlock new markets.

Highlight: The Bulls’ Spanish-language Strategy

Launch of @LosBulls — In September 2024, the Bulls unveiled a dedicated Spanish-language Instagram account, @LosBulls, aimed at reaching Hispanic audiences worldwide.

Original, culturally-relevant content — The account doesn’t just translate content; it publishes bespoke posts in Spanish, featuring former and current players engaging directly with the Latino fan base.

Rapid growth and impact — Within its first months, @LosBulls grew significantly, signalling appetite and engagement from Spanish-speaking communities globally and local communities.

Deep Dive — What Makes the Bulls’ Approach Worth Watching

  • It respects cultural nuance, not just language. Instead of auto-translating English posts, the Bulls created unique content tailored to Hispanic fans: in-language captioning, culturally relevant references, and even collaborations with Latin American idols.
  • It builds a dedicated funnel, not just broadcast. @LosBulls isn’t a secondary channel — it’s positioned as a primary touchpoint for Spanish-speaking fans. This dedicated presence helps the club segment its global audience appropriately, rather than lumping all fans into one “global” feed.
  • It leverages community & identity to expand fandom. The branding, tone, and content formats create a sense of belonging. For many fans outside the U.S., it’s the first time they feel directly addressed as part of the Bulls family — beyond language barriers.

It opens doors for long-term partnerships and monetization. By demonstrating commitment to international fans, the Bulls improve opportunities for global sponsorships and collaborations tailored to Latin markets.

Tip of the Week

If you manage a club or sports brand: don’t treat new languages or markets as translation zones.

  • Launch dedicated regional channels when there’s a significant fan base abroad.

  • Produce original content in the local language, rooted in local culture and storytelling.

Use region-specific ambassadors or relatable athletes to build authenticity.

That way you build relationships — not just broadcasts.
