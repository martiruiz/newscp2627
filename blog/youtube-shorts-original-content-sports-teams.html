Sports Social Media Trends: YouTube Shorts now rewards original content, what it means for sports teams

By Martí Ruiz · 15 October 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • On 1 October, TeamYouTube confirmed that Shorts recommendations will now prioritise original content over re-uploads.
  • YouTube evaluates originality at channel level, and it has not published any threshold that keeps a channel safe.
  • Official club channels have a natural advantage because they have access that fan pages and aggregators lack.
  • FC Barcelona's shirt lettering Short reached 1.1 million views in its first days, around 9 times the channel's usual performance according to vidIQ.
  • Clubs do not need to drop highlights; they need to wrap them in process clips, their own angles and player voice, planned ahead in a content calendar.

On 1 October, TeamYouTube confirmed a change that every club social media manager should note: Shorts recommendations will now prioritise original content. Channels that mostly re-upload other people's videos without adding anything new will see their reach reduced. It is one of the clearest sports social media trends of the season, and it rewards exactly what clubs already own: access.

What YouTube changed and why it shapes sports social media trends

According to the announcement, the system evaluates originality at channel level, not only clip by clip. YouTube has not published thresholds, so there is no magic percentage of original posts that keeps a channel safe. The message is simple: if your Shorts feed is mostly highlights ripped from broadcasts or trends copied frame by frame, the algorithm will show it to fewer people.

For sports, this matters because many fan pages and aggregator accounts grew by reposting clips. Official club channels have a natural advantage, but only if they publish material nobody else can.

What the profiles that stand out have in common

Look at the sports accounts that keep growing on Shorts, Reels and TikTok and a pattern appears. They rarely win with the same clip everyone else has. They win with access: the dressing room, the kit room, the training ground, the voice of the player. Their feeds feel like a backstage pass, not a replay of the broadcast.

FC Barcelona's Shorts are a good illustration. A simple process video, a craftsman lettering a player's name and number on a shirt, reached 1.1 million views in its first days, around 9 times the channel's usual performance according to vidIQ, and has kept growing since.

It is not a one-off. A training-ground challenge, “Who will survive until the end?”, performed around 3 times above the channel average in the same week. Different format, same ingredient: moments only the club can film.

The lesson is not one video. It is the strategy behind the whole feed: a steady flow of pieces that only you can produce. The grid below shows how the access-driven Shorts compare with the rest. That is the thinking behind the best football social media content ideas today.

Frequently asked questions

What did YouTube change about Shorts recommendations?

On 1 October, TeamYouTube confirmed that Shorts recommendations will prioritise original content. Channels that mostly re-upload other people's videos without adding anything new will see their reach reduced.

Does YouTube judge originality per video or per channel?

According to the announcement, originality is evaluated at channel level, not only clip by clip. YouTube has not published thresholds, so there is no set percentage of original posts that keeps a channel safe.

Should sports teams stop posting highlights on YouTube Shorts?

No. The update does not mean abandoning highlights, but wrapping them in something only the club can add. Tunnel cams, bench reactions, dressing-room celebrations or a short reaction from the scorer add value the TV feed cannot.

What kind of original Shorts work best for sports clubs?

Content built on access tends to stand out: the dressing room, the kit room, the training ground and the player's voice.
