Social media ideas for sports teams: film the guests, not just the game

By Martí Ruiz · 23 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • An event has two stories: the competition and what happens around it.
  • Guests and culture bring a second audience that the score alone cannot reach.
  • Co-publishing with the guest turns one Reel into two audiences.
  • Plan the surroundings in your content calendar and assign someone to film them.
  • Measure results against your own median to show value to sponsors.

Which Reel got more views: the comeback goal or a greeting before kick-off? In the daily analysis of the Barça account, the greeting won by three to one. Lamine Yamal welcoming the artist Ryan Castro at the Camp Nou reached 21.2 million views in about 19 hours, against 7.2 million for Fermín's comeback goal. That is a strong signal for anyone collecting social media ideas for sports teams: the result gives you the news, but the people and the culture around the match give you the reach.

The same weekend, Formula 1 showed the same pattern in Las Vegas. This post explains what both accounts did, why it works, and how a club, a league or a brand can build its own version, even without a famous guest.

What the daily analysis showed

The data comes from the daily analysis of the Instagram accounts of FC Barcelona and Formula 1, plus match and race reports from the same weekend.

  • Barça's Reel with Lamine Yamal and Ryan Castro before the match reached 21.2 million views in about 19 hours.
  • That is three times the comeback goal by Fermín (7.2 million views) and 1.8 times the account's median of 11.95 million.
  • The Reel was also signed by the artist, so it reached his audience too, a door that football does not usually open.
  • On the F1 account, the Reel of Max Verstappen's reaction after his 75th win reached 17.8 million views and 33,000 shares. That is 4.3 times the median of 26 Reels from the weekend.
  • Around the race, a Reel of Keanu Reeves driving the podium limousine reached 7.2 million views, and one of Chris Pratt lowering the chequered flag reached 5.2 million.

A caution: these are figures from one analysis day, and Reels keep collecting views for days. Read them as a direction, not as a final ranking. Also, big clubs and F1 have audiences most teams do not have. The useful number is the ratio against the profile's own median.

Social media ideas for sports teams: every event has two stories

In both cases, the account told two stories. The first is the competition: the goal, the win, the result. The second is what happens around it: who arrives, who is in the stand, who stands next to the trophy, how a driver reacts when he cannot believe what he just did.

The competition gives the peak

The result still matters. Fans want the goal and the win, and they will look for them. But everyone publishes the result, so it is the hardest piece to stand out with.

The surroundings give the reach

The second story is where the profile can be different. A guest brings a second audience. An emotion brings shares. A person gives the viewer a reason to send the video to a friend. In the Las Vegas example, the face of the winner got far more attention than a standard race summary would be expected to get, and the celebrity moments filled the rest of the weekend.

Two stories, told separately

The lesson is not to replace the match with guests. It is to publish both and to keep them apart. One piece for the competition, other pieces for the show. That is a clear sports social media strategy: the match sets the peak, the world around it fills the week.

Why guests and culture travel further than the score

  • A new audience arrives. An artist, an actor or a local figure follows other accounts. When they co-publish or share, their fans see your club for the first time.
  • Two idols in one frame. Fans of the player and fans of the artist both have a reason to watch and share.
  • It is easy to understand. A greeting needs no context. You do not need to know the league table to enjoy it.
  • It is hard to copy. Anyone can cut a goal. Only the host club has the access to the tunnel, the pitch and the person.

This fits one of the clearest sports social media trends of the moment: accounts that stand out use sport as a stage for culture, not only as a subject.

Ten sports content ideas around the game

You do not need Hollywood. These sports content ideas work for a first-division club, a regional federation or a small team with a local audience.

  1. The local artist on the pitch. Invite a musician from your city to walk out with the team or to meet one player before the match. Film the first greeting.
  2. The idol meets the idol. A young fan who loves your captain, a veteran who once played for the club, an athlete from another sport. Show the first ten seconds of the meeting.
  3. The guest in the stand. A chef, an actor or a comedian from your area watching the match. Ask one question: what do you feel when the stadium sings?
  4. The ceremonial moment. Who delivers the ball, who rings the bell, who lowers the flag. Give that person a Reel of their own.
  5. The winner's face. After a big win, publish the reaction, not only the summary. One person, one sentence, one close-up.
  6. The podium or tunnel side story. Who drove the car, who carried the trophy, who waited at the door. Small roles become content.
  7. The pre-match walk. Arrivals, outfits and hugs. Pair them with a song from the guest.
  8. The fan's reaction to a guest. Film the people in the stand when the guest appears. Their reaction is the story.
  9. The co-authored goodbye. After the match, a short Reel co-published with the guest, thanking the stadium.
  10. The thing nobody is filming. Walk through your stadium two hours before kick-off and write down what nobody records. That list is your next ten Reels.

How to choose the right guest

  • Pick someone your fans already talk about, not someone only the sponsor likes.
  • Check that the guest has an audience that does not already follow your club.
  • Agree in advance who can publish, who co-authors and at what time.
  • Keep the format simple: one meeting, one moment, one caption.

How to adapt it by platform

The same raw footage can serve several platforms if you cut it with a purpose.

  • Instagram: the main piece. Co-publish it with the guest. These are the base of your sports Instagram content ideas for the matchday, with Stories for the quick reactions.
  • TikTok: shorter and less polished. For sports TikTok content ideas, start with the most surprising second: the hug, the laugh, the song. Skip heavy graphics.
  • YouTube Shorts and X: reuse the strongest cut and change the caption. Keep the same protagonist.

For sports reels ideas, the rule is the same on every platform: first second, one person, one feeling. If you want a football-specific starting point, these football social media content ideas work well on a match day: the guest greeting before kick-off, the stand reaction at the first goal and the thank-you after the final whistle.

How to plan the surroundings before the event

The best surrounding moments are not improvised. Someone has to ask, agree and film. Three habits make this easy.

Put the guests in the calendar

Add the match or event week to your sports content calendar. Give one slot to the competition and one or two to the show. Write the guest, the format and the person who films. Leave one slot open for what happens.

Assign a person to film the things nobody films

A sports content planning tool helps assign roles: who talks to the guest, who films the arrival, who has permission to publish, and who answers the comments. In a match week, speed depends on that.

Use tools to speed up, not to decide

Good sports content creation tools help trim, caption and resize the clips for several platforms. AI tools for sports content creation can suggest subtitles or a first caption, but a person must check names, spelling and the guest's wishes. With an external guest, one mistake is more costly than an hour of editing.

Measure against your own median

The Barça Reel stood out because it was 1.8 times the account median, not only because it was large. Do the same with your numbers. Then use your sports marketing tools to put that ratio in a short report for partners. A guest piece with a clear ratio is easy to present to sponsors, and it shows value beyond the result.

Takeaway

The pattern is clear. The accounts that stand out do not only publish what happened in the competition. They also publish who was there and how it felt. Barça and F1 told two stories in the same weekend, and the second one travelled further. Before your next match, write down who will be in your stadium, who will meet whom, and what nobody is filming yet. That short list is the start of your next piece.

Frequently asked questions

Why did a pre-match greeting beat a comeback goal?

It put two idols in one frame, needed no context and reached the artist's audience too, while the goal is a piece every account publishes.

Do small clubs need celebrity guests?

No. A local musician, a veteran player or a fan with a story can work, as long as they bring an audience that does not yet follow you.

Should guest content replace match content?

No. Publish the competition and the surroundings as two separate stories, so each one has its own piece.

How do I know if a guest piece worked?

Compare its views and shares with your profile's median, not with other accounts, and note the ratio for your reports.
