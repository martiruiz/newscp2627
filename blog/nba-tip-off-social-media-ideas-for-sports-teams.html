Sports content ideas

From schedule drop to tip-off: social media ideas for sports teams from the NBA build-up

By Martí Ruiz · 18 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • The NBA's best social media teams treat every predictable off-season milestone, from the schedule release to media day, as a planned content event.
  • Strong schedule releases start with a concept, such as a partner crossover, a player mini-film or a community collaboration, and use the dates as the excuse.
  • Media day is the most efficient content day of the year: tease it in advance, shoot duo and trio pairings, and hand players the account or the camera.
  • One idea should be adapted to each platform, for example a vertical reel, a quick X clip and a carousel of stills, rather than posted identically everywhere.
  • Access-based content that feels unscripted, such as player takeovers and role reversals, travels further than polished promos.

The 2026-27 NBA season tips off on Tuesday, 20 October, with the Knicks raising their first championship banner since 1973 against a rebuilt 76ers side. But for the 30 teams' social media teams, the season started weeks ago. Between the schedule release in August and media day in late September, NBA clubs ran a two-month build-up that is one of the best sources of social media ideas for sports teams in any league. The scale explains why: the NBA says it generated a record 228 billion social media views in 2025-26, up 13% year on year.

This post is not about a single viral clip. It is about the pattern behind the teams that stand out: they treat every off-season milestone as a content event, they put players and partners at the centre, and they adapt each idea to the platform where it lives. Here is how that works, with examples you can replicate at any level, from a basketball franchise to a football club or a federation.

Why the build-up matters more than opening night

Most clubs plan heavily for matchday and lightly for everything before it. The NBA's best accounts do the opposite. They know opening night will get attention anyway, so the real work is giving fans a reason to care in the quiet weeks: a schedule drop, a new signing in a new jersey, the first photo of the squad together. Each of those moments is predictable, which means each one can be planned weeks ahead in a sports content calendar.

That is the first lesson of a good sports social media strategy: the dates you already know are your biggest creative advantage. Schedule release, kit launch, pre-season camp, media day, first home game. If they are on the calendar in June, the ideas can be scripted, shot and approved long before the deadline.

Schedule release: turning a list of dates into entertainment

A schedule is, by nature, the least exciting asset a club owns: 82 games, dates and opponents. ESPN's roundup of the 13 August release shows how NBA teams turned that list into short entertainment pieces. Three approaches stood out.

1. The crossover with a partner from outside sport

The Toronto Raptors gave their schedule "the Chelsea Green treatment", a WWE-style makeover with the wrestler herself. The post had 6,394 likes on X at the time of writing, one of the strongest results among the releases we checked. The reason it works: it borrows an audience and a tone (wrestling's over-the-top drama) that already lives on social, and it gives the partner a reason to share it too.

2. The mini-film starring players

The LA Clippers released "The Lesson", a movie-style parody starring Keaton Wagler and Curry Barker, presented by Ticketmaster. It reached 3,996 likes on X. The format solves two problems at once: players become characters fans want to follow all season, and the sponsor gets an integration that feels like part of the joke, not an ad break.

3. The purpose-led release

The Memphis Grizzlies took a different route: their schedule was drawn in partnership with Le Bonheur Children's Hospital and presented as a piece of art. It is not chasing the biggest numbers (484 likes on X), and that is the point. Not every post has to be a reach play. A release that involves the community gives the club a story the local press, the partner and families will share, and it builds trust that pays off over a whole season.

The common thread: none of these teams simply posted the dates. They chose a concept first, then used the schedule as the excuse. That is the mindset behind the best sports content ideas.

Media day: one shoot, a week of content

Media day, held on 28 September, is the single most efficient content day of the year. Every player is in the building, in the new kit, with time blocked for cameras. ESPN's media day roundup collected dozens of posts across Instagram and X, and the strongest ones share a structure.

First, they teased it. In the run-up, the Grizzlies posted a "Summer Achievement Cake" reel, with players adding their biggest off-season achievement. A teaser like this turns media day into an event fans wait for, not just a photo dump.

Second, they built posts around relationships, not portraits. The Lakers' post of JJ Redick, Luka Dončić and Austin Reaves together, captioned "these three are excited to be back", reached 4,689 likes on X. A group shot with chemistry tells fans more about the season than three separate headshots.

Third, they handed over the keys. The Denver Nuggets let players run the team account on X throughout the day, signing posts with their names. The Nuggets' "Hi I'm Zeke" is as simple as it gets and still collected 752 likes, because fans read it as the player's voice, not the club's.

And the Indiana Pacers put Pascal Siakam behind the camera, turning their star into the photographer. Role reversal is a cheap, repeatable trick that gives behind-the-scenes content a new angle.

The pattern: concept first, then platform

Look at the examples side by side and the strategy becomes clear. On X, teams used short text plus video for speed and banter. On Instagram, they leaned on reels with players talking, reacting and playing up their personalities. The same idea is rarely posted identically everywhere. That is why the best sports Instagram content ideas and sports TikTok content ideas usually start as one concept and are cut three ways: a vertical reel, a quick X clip, a carousel of stills.

It also explains why sports reels ideas built on access (a player taking photos, a takeover, a cake of achievements) travel further than polished promos. They feel unscripted even when they are carefully planned. This is one of the clearest sports social media trends of the season, and it is not limited to basketball.

Social media ideas for sports teams to replicate before your next season

You do not need an NBA budget to copy the structure. Here is a practical list, ready to drop into your planning:

  • Fixture release with a concept: pick one theme (a film parody, a game show, a local legend) and use the dates as the punchline. This works just as well in football: the best football social media content ideas for fixture day follow the same logic.
  • Partner crossover: invite a personality from outside sport (music, wrestling, comedy) and give them a reason to share the post with their audience.
  • Community version: let a local school, hospital or fan group design one asset of the release.
  • Media day teaser: post a playful preview 24 hours before, so the shoot becomes an event.
  • Duo and trio shots: plan pairings around new signings and key partnerships instead of single portraits.
  • Player takeover: give two or three players the account for an hour, with clear guidelines and their names signed under each post.
  • Role reversal: put a player behind the camera or the microphone for behind-the-scenes clips.
  • Sponsor inside the story: write the partner into the script, as the Clippers did with Ticketmaster, rather than adding a logo at the end.

Plan these on your calendar with owners and deadlines, and you will have a month of sports social media content ideas before a ball is kicked.

Frequently asked questions

What are good social media ideas for sports teams before the season starts?

Plan content around the dates you already know: fixture or schedule release, kit launch, pre-season camp, media day and the first home game. The NBA's best accounts turned each of these into a content event with a concept, players and partners at the centre.

How can a sports team make its schedule release more engaging?

Pick a concept first and use the dates as the punchline. NBA examples include the Raptors' WWE-style release with Chelsea Green, the Clippers' mini-film starring players and presented by Ticketmaster, and the Grizzlies' schedule drawn with Le Bonheur Children's Hospital.

What content should a team create on media day?

Tease the shoot 24 hours before so it feels like an event, then plan duo and trio shots instead of single portraits. Player takeovers of the team account and role reversals, like the Pacers putting Pascal Siakam behind the camera, add behind-the-scenes angles.

Should sports teams post the same content on every platform?

No. The strongest NBA accounts used short text plus video on X for speed and banter, and reels with players talking and reacting on Instagram. One concept is usually cut several ways, such as a vertical reel, a quick X clip and a carousel of stills.
