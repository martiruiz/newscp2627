The smart way to keep sponsors visible in social media in sports without ruining the content

By Josep · 18 April 2026 · 3 min read

Most branded content in social media in sports follows the same pattern: a quick logo hit at the start, then it disappears to avoid disrupting the fan experience.


Clean? Yes. Effective? Not always.

The problem is simple – brands pay for visibility, but often get just a few seconds of attention.


Olympique Lyonnais, through their “Views from the Pitch” series presented by eToro, offers a smarter alternative. One that balances fan experience and sponsor value without compromising either.

This article breaks down what they did right, where most teams fail, and how any content creator can replicate this approach immediately.

The OL x eToro approach: subtle but strategic branding

In the “Views from the Pitch” series, the opening animation follows a clear sequence:

  • The full series lockup appears front and center
  • The branding establishes instantly
  • Then it transitions out of the main focus


Instead of disappearing completely, the eToro logo moves to the bottom of the screen and stays visible throughout the content.

This changes everything.

The result:

  • The fan gets a clean, unobstructed view
  • The sponsor maintains constant visibility
  • The integration feels natural, not intrusive

This is a perfect example of how small design decisions in digital content can have a big impact on both fan engagement and commercial value.

Why most teams get this wrong in social media in sports

The traditional model is built around caution.

Teams are afraid of:

  • Over-branding
  • Hurting the viewing experience
  • Making content feel like an ad

So they default to:

  • 2–3 seconds of logo exposure
  • No persistent branding
  • Minimal integration

The issue?

This approach undervalues the asset. From a sports marketing perspective, it creates:

For a content creator, it also limits creative possibilities. You’re essentially separating content and branding instead of merging them.

OL flips that mindset.

The design insight: simplify to scale visibility

One of the most interesting aspects of this case is not just the placement – it’s the design system behind it.

The problem with traditional lockups

Many series lockups are:

  • Too complex
  • Too long
  • Not designed for flexible placement

This leads to:

  • Tiny, unreadable logos
  • Cluttered visuals
  • Poor mobile experience

OL’s solution

OL simplifies the visual hierarchy:

  1. Establish the full identity at the start
  2. Remove the heavy elements
  3. Keep a clean, readable brand mark on screen

This creates:

  • More time on screen for the sponsor
  • Better visual clarity
  • A more premium content feel

It’s not about adding more branding. It’s about designing smarter branding.

The impact on fan engagement and brand value

This approach directly improves both sides of the equation.

For fans

  • Less intrusive viewing experience
  • Cleaner visuals
  • Higher content quality perception

For sponsors

  • Continuous visibility
  • Stronger association with the content
  • Better brand recall

In social media in sports, this balance is key.

Fans don’t want ads. But they accept branding when it feels integrated into the content experience.

That’s where real audience growth happens.

How to apply this as a content creator

You don’t need a big production team to implement this.

Here’s a simple framework any content creator or club can use:

1. Rethink your intro sequence

  • Start with a clear brand + series identification
  • Keep it short but impactful

2. Design for persistence

  • Create a version of the logo that works in corners
  • Ensure readability on mobile

3. Separate identity from presence

  • Use full lockup only at the start
  • Maintain a simplified brand element during the content

4. Test placement and opacity

  • Bottom corners usually work best
  • Adjust size to avoid distraction

5. Measure performance

  • Track retention rates
  • Compare engagement with and without persistent branding

It’s about making better decisions, faster.

Common mistakes to avoid

Even when trying to improve branding, teams often fall into these traps:

  • Overloading the screen with logos
  • Using unreadable brand assets
  • Ignoring mobile-first design
  • Treating all content formats the same


The key is balance. OL’s execution works because it respects both the fan and the sponsor.

Tip of the week

Design a “corner-safe” version of your sponsor logo and test it in your next video series to increase visibility without affecting the viewing experience.
