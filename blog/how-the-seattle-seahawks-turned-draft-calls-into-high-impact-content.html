How the Seattle Seahawks turned draft calls into high-impact content

By Josep · 2 May 2026 · 3 min read

Every year, the NFL Draft generates massive attention. Yet most teams communicate it in the same way: static graphics, highlight clips, and reposted broadcast moments.

The result? Content that looks identical across franchises and struggles to stand out in crowded feeds.

The Seattle Seahawks took a different approach. Instead of focusing only on the selection itself, they built their content strategy around the most emotional moment of the process: the phone call to the player.

This shift is small in execution, but big in impact. And it offers a clear lesson for any club, agency, or content creator working in social media in sports.

The real insight: the moment is not the pick, it’s the call

Most teams treat the draft pick as the content. Seattle reframed it. They understood that the real story is the human reaction when a player finds out their life is about to change.

By sharing the calls on Instagram, they captured:

  • Raw emotion
  • Unfiltered reactions
  • Family context
  • Personality of both player and staff
  • A sense of exclusivity for the fan

This creates something traditional formats cannot: emotional proximity.

Fans are not just informed. They feel part of the moment.

For any content creator, this is a critical shift: stop documenting outcomes, start capturing reactions.

Differentiation in a saturated content moment

Draft day is one of the most saturated content events in sports. Every team publishes:

  • Pick announcements
  • Player highlights
  • Branded templates
  • Press conference clips

This creates a uniform content landscape. Seattle broke that pattern by focusing on a format others were not prioritizing.

Key advantages of their approach:

1. Scroll-stopping content

A phone call thumbnail already creates curiosity.

2. Narrative depth

Each video tells a slightly different story.

3. Series potential

Every pick becomes an episode.

4. Human connection

Fans connect faster with voices and reactions than with graphics.

This is how you win attention in modern sports marketing: not by posting more, but by posting differently.

Extending the story beyond social media

The Seahawks did not stop at Instagram. They expanded the experience through their “Inside the Draft Room” section on their website, giving fans access to:

  • Decision-making conversations
  • Internal dynamics
  • Behind-the-scenes processes
  • Organizational personality

This is a key strategic move. Most teams treat social media as the final destination. Seattle uses it as an entry point.

This creates a multi-platform ecosystem:

  • Social = discovery
  • Website = depth
  • Content series = retention

For audience growth, this is far more effective than isolated posts.

What they could still improve

Even strong executions have opportunities. From a strategic perspective, there are three areas to optimize:

Lack of pre-built narrative

The calls are powerful, but there is limited setup before the moment.

Opportunity:

  • Tease potential picks
  • Introduce draft needs
  • Build anticipation

Limited player storytelling post-call

The emotional peak happens during the call, but follow-up content could deepen the story.

Opportunity:

  • “First 24 hours as a Seahawk”
  • Family reactions
  • First interaction with teammates

Monetization potential

Currently, the format is strong editorially but underleveraged commercially.

Opportunity:

  • Partner integration in the call moment (subtle)
  • Sponsored “welcome to the league” series
  • Branded behind-the-scenes access

These optimizations can transform good content into a long-term asset.

A framework to replicate this strategy

This model can be applied across sports, not just drafts.

1. Identify hidden emotional moments

Look beyond the obvious event:

  • Contract signings
  • Call-ups from academy
  • Injury comebacks
  • Debut announcements

2. Capture the first reaction

The first reaction is always the most powerful.

Prioritize:

  • Audio
  • Face
  • Environment
  • Real-time context

3. Turn moments into formats

Do not treat it as one-off content.

Build a repeatable series:

  • “The call”
  • “The moment”
  • “You made it”

4. Expand across platforms

Think beyond one post:

  • Short-form video (social)
  • Long-form (YouTube or web)
  • Written storytelling (website)

5. Add strategic layers

Once the format works:

  • Introduce sponsors naturally
  • Add narrative arcs
  • Build seasonal consistency

Why this matters for fan engagement

This type of content directly impacts how fans relate to a club. Instead of seeing players as assets, they see them as people.

That drives:

  • Higher engagement rates
  • More shares and saves
  • Stronger emotional connection
  • Better long-term loyalty

In modern social media in sports, emotional storytelling outperforms informational content almost every time.

Tip of the week

Next time you plan a content piece, ask: What is the most emotional micro-moment inside this event? Focus on that, not the headline outcome.
