Social media in sports: how an unknown footballer became one of the World Cup’s biggest stories

By Josep · 6 June 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • New Zealand defender Tim Payne went from roughly 4,000 to more than 4 million Instagram followers within days during the World Cup.
  • The movement started when Argentine creator Valentín Scarsini identified Payne as the least-followed player at the World Cup and asked his followers to follow him.
  • The campaign worked because it was simple, easy to join and built on empathy and a sense of collective participation rather than on-pitch performance.
  • Payne's genuine thank-you video, including his efforts to learn Spanish on Duolingo, turned him into a relatable personality and kept the momentum going.
  • For sports organizations, the lesson is to prioritize participation, simplicity and authenticity over production quality, and to let the community lead.

Every major tournament creates unexpected stars. Usually, those players become famous because of goals, performances, or dramatic moments on the pitch. Tim Payne’s rise, one of the biggest stories in social media in sports, was completely different.

The New Zealand defender arrived at the World Cup as one of the least-known players in the competition. Within days, he went from roughly 4,000 Instagram followers to more than 4 million, becoming one of the most talked-about names across football social media.

What makes this story fascinating is not just the growth itself. It is how a simple idea, launched by a creator, evolved into a global digital movement involving fans, musicians, national teams, media outlets, and even FIFA.

For anyone working in social media in sports, this case offers valuable lessons about community-driven storytelling, audience behavior, and the power of authenticity.

The campaign started with a simple observation

The phenomenon began when Argentine creator Valentín Scarsini decided to answer a curious question: Who was the least-followed player at the World Cup?

After reviewing the participants, he identified Tim Payne as the player with the smallest social media audience. Instead of simply sharing the information, he turned it into a mission. His followers were encouraged to support Payne by following his Instagram account.

The concept was incredibly simple:

  • Easy to understand
  • Easy to participate in
  • Easy to share
  • Easy to explain to others

Within less than 24 hours, Payne gained hundreds of thousands of followers. Soon after, the story expanded far beyond Argentina.

Why social media in sports embraced Tim Payne

Most viral sports stories are built around performance. This one was built around empathy. Fans were not following Payne because he was expected to win the Golden Boot or dominate headlines. They followed him because they felt part of a collective project.

The campaign created a sense of belonging. Users could actively contribute to the story simply by clicking a button. This is a powerful lesson for every content creator: People engage more when they feel they can influence the narrative. The growth became a game.

Every milestone generated new content:

  • 100,000 followers
  • 500,000 followers
  • 1 million followers
  • 4 million followers

The audience was not just consuming the story. They were helping create it.

Authenticity became the fuel of the movement

Many viral campaigns disappear quickly because they lack a human connection. Payne’s reaction changed everything. Instead of ignoring the attention, he embraced it.

His thank-you video became one of the key moments of the story. By speaking directly to fans and even mentioning his efforts to learn Spanish through Duolingo, he transformed from an unknown footballer into a relatable personality.

This was critical. The internet loves authenticity. The moment fans realized Payne was genuinely grateful and engaged, the campaign became more sustainable.

That emotional connection accelerated fan engagement and encouraged even more sharing across platforms.

The ecosystem effect: when everyone joins the story

One of the most interesting aspects of the campaign was how it spread across multiple communities. The story moved from football audiences into:

  • TikTok communities
  • Music communities
  • Meme accounts
  • Football media
  • National team fanbases

Argentine musician Ca7riel even released a song inspired by Payne. His wife appeared in videos reacting to the trend.

FIFA created dedicated content around the defender. Even New Zealand rugby’s All Blacks joined the conversation.

This is what makes modern digital content so powerful. Once a story becomes culturally relevant, it stops belonging to one platform.

It becomes an ecosystem.

What brands and sports organizations can learn

Many sports organizations spend months planning campaigns that fail to generate a fraction of this attention. Why?

Because they often prioritize production quality over participation. The Tim Payne phenomenon followed a different formula.

1. Build around a simple idea

The best campaigns can be explained in one sentence. In this case: «Let’s make the least-followed World Cup player famous.»

2. Make participation easy

No forms. No contests. No barriers. Just follow.

3. Let the community lead

Fans became the distribution engine.

4. Embrace the unexpected

The campaign evolved naturally through creators, musicians, media outlets, and sports organizations. Trying to control it would have weakened it.

The opportunities Tim Payne’s team should explore

While the growth was extraordinary, there are opportunities to turn temporary attention into long-term value.

Storytelling content

Fans now want to know more about him. Potential formats include:

  • Day-in-the-life videos
  • Behind-the-scenes with New Zealand
  • Family content
  • Training routines

Collaborations

The campaign has already connected with creators and musicians. Additional partnerships could expand his audience further.

Community engagement

Q&A sessions, fan challenges, and interactive content would help maintain momentum after the World Cup. The biggest challenge is no longer gaining attention. It is keeping it.

Why this matters for social media in sports

The Tim Payne story is a reminder that audiences are not always looking for stars. Sometimes they are looking for stories. The campaign succeeded because it combined:

  • Simplicity
  • Authenticity
  • Participation
  • Community
  • Emotional connection

These are often stronger drivers of audience growth than performance alone. In a digital landscape crowded with polished content, genuine human stories remain one of the most powerful assets available to sports organizations.
