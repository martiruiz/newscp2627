How to search and filter content ideas

Content LibraryUpdated 12 November 2026 · 2 min read

In the Sports Content Playbook library you can type words or a whole sentence in the search box, which turns them into filters for free, then refine with the smart filters in the left column (What works, Match Day, Brands & sponsors, Where & how, Inspiration from), a period and a sort. Save any combination as a saved search or share it as a link.

Search: words or whole sentences

The search box at the top of the library says Search: signings, a club, reels…. You can type a single word, a club name or a format. You can also type a sentence such as "transfer reels with a sponsor that worked": Sports Content Playbook turns your words into filters. Sentence search is free and does not use your AI analyses. Suggestion chips under the box offer common searches.

Tip: type "top picks" to see the editorial selection of the best posts chosen by the SCP team; it is not a button in the filter column.

The smart filters

AI labels every post, and those labels become the filters in the left column:

  • What works: Favs, Replicable ideas, Content Creators, Overperforming, Views, Publisher type.
  • Match Day: Pre-match / Live / Post-match, Result, Opponent, Transfers, Kit, Season moment, Campaign moment, Angle and tone (and more: Press and comms, Sensitive topics).
  • Brands & sponsors (Pro): Business goal, Sponsors, Integration, Sponsor sector (and Call to action, Collaborations).
  • Where & how: Format, Shape, Length, Language, Platform.
  • Inspiration from: Challenges, Sport, Competition, Club, Team (and City).

Content Creators (from Plus) has its own filters: creator type, language, country, gender, followers and accounts. Match Day and Brands & sponsors filters are detected by AI on Instagram posts from October 2026; older posts are still being analysed.

Step by step: build, save and share a search

  1. Type a sentence in the search box, or start with one filter in the left column.
  2. Add filters from other sections. Active filters appear as chips above the feed.
  3. Choose a period: This week, This month, This season, All time or Custom dates…
  4. Choose a sort: Latest, Most viewed or Surprise me.
  5. Click Save search and give it a name. Reopen it any time from Saved searches…
  6. To share it, use Copy link to this search: filters live in the URL, so a colleague with access sees the same results.
  7. Start again with Clear all.

Example: post-match reels after a win

A second-division football club wins away and wants football social media content ideas for the next morning. The manager types "post-match reels", then in Match Day picks the win result and in Inspiration from her competition. She sets This season and sorts by Most viewed. She saves it as "Post-match win" so the next time the team wins, the sports reels ideas are one click away. To spot sports social media trends, she reopens the same search with This week and Latest.

Tips and limits

  • Start broad, then narrow. Two or three filters usually beat six; with too many you may see "No posts match these filters."
  • Click a tag inside a post's Tags and caption to filter the library by it.
  • Use Favs and Clubs like mine after setting favourites in Profile to see sports content ideas from accounts like yours first.
  • Plan access: Content Creators from Plus, Brands & sponsors in Pro; Match Day in every plan.

Frequently asked questions

Does sentence search use my AI analyses?

No. Turning a sentence into filters is free. Only unlocking an AI analysis of a post uses your allowance.

How do I share a search with my team?

Filters live in the URL. Use Copy link to this search and send it; anyone with access to the library sees the same results.

Why don't I see Match Day filters on some older posts?

Match Day and Brands & sponsors labels are detected by AI on Instagram posts from October 2026; older posts are still being analysed.

Where are Top Picks?

Type "top picks" in the search box. It is an editorial selection by the SCP team, not a button in the filter column.
