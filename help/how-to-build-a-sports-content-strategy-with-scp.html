How to build a successful sports content strategy with SCP

Tips & Best PracticesUpdated 12 November 2026 · 3 min read

With Sports Content Playbook you build a sports social media strategy in five steps: set your profile so the library shows clubs like yours, study what overperforms in your sport and competition, turn the formats that fit you into a weekly base of recurring posts, add key dates to your content calendar, and review the library every month to update the plan.

What a content strategy looks like in SCP

A sports social media strategy answers three questions: what you post, how often, and why it should work. Sports Content Playbook helps with the third one, which is usually the hardest: instead of guessing, you look at real posts from 770+ clubs and 110+ competitions, see which ones performed above their account's usual, and read the AI analysis of why.

SCP uses public posts only. You don't connect your own social accounts, so it won't report your own results; use your networks' native statistics for that. SCP's job is to give you proven sports content ideas and a place to plan them.

Build your strategy step by step

  1. Set your reference group. In Profile, fill in Your sport, your favourite network, 5 favourite profiles (clubs, leagues, brands) and 5 favourite creators. This powers Favs, Clubs like mine and Fill my week.
  2. Study what works. In the Content Library, combine Overperforming (posts with at least 2x their account's median that quarter) with your Sport and Competition, and set the period to This season. Save the strongest posts to a board called “Strategy”.
  3. Find your content pillars. Look for repeated patterns: the Match Day timeline (Pre-match, Live, Post-match), Transfers, Kit, Season moment, or Brands & sponsors on Pro. Pick 3 to 5 types you can produce every week. Replicable ideas shows formats you can recreate with your own players and venue.
  4. Understand the why. Run an AI analysis on one post per pillar. Read Why it works, the Hook and How to adapt it to your club, and note the reusable template.
  5. Turn pillars into a weekly base. In the Content Plan (Plus and Pro), add each pillar to the Weekly base. These recurring posts show as red placeholders until you plan them.
  6. Add key dates. The Content Plan includes 12 key dates (Pre-season, Fixture release, Halloween, Black Friday, Christmas…); each opens the library filtered to that topic. For a full season view, download the free Sports Content Calendar 2026-27 PDF in Free resources.
  7. Review every month. Re-run your saved search for Overperforming posts. Overperforming is recomputed every hour, so new winners appear quickly.

Example: a handball federation

A handball federation runs the accounts of its national teams and its league. The communications lead sets Handball as the sport and five federations and clubs she admires as favourites. In Overperforming for this season she notices that post-match player reactions and short training challenges keep beating their accounts' median. She also filters by Challenges, because every Challenges post is also Replicable.

She defines four pillars: match preview carousel, post-match reaction reel, weekly challenge, and a sponsor post. She analyses one example of each, then builds a Weekly base: preview on Thursday, challenge on Tuesday, reaction after each match, sponsor post on Friday. Before Christmas and the fixture release she drags key-date ideas into the No date tray. Every month she checks Overperforming again and swaps one pillar if a new format is clearly winning. These social media ideas for sports teams become a repeatable plan rather than a weekly scramble.

Common mistakes

  • Copying the biggest clubs only. A post with millions of views from a giant club may not fit your resources. Overperforming compares each post with its own account, so smaller clubs that punch above their weight show up too. Add Clubs like mine to stay close to your level.
  • Too many pillars. Five recurring posts you can actually produce beat ten you abandon in a month.
  • Spending AI analyses on everything. Your allowance is monthly (5, 10 or 30). Use filters and the post's own metrics first, then analyse the posts you will really adapt.
  • Planning without a calendar. A sports content calendar with key dates avoids last-minute posts for Christmas or the start of the season.

Frequently asked questions

Does SCP measure the results of my own posts?

No. Sports Content Playbook works with public posts and you don't connect your accounts. Use your networks' own statistics to track your results and SCP to find what to post next.

How often should I review my strategy?

Once a month works well: re-run your saved Overperforming search for your sport and competition and update your weekly base if a new format is clearly winning.

Can I plan key dates without the Content Plan?

Yes. The free Sports Content Calendar 2026-27 PDF in Free resources lists 28 dates for 9 sports. The Content Plan, on Plus and Pro, adds 12 key dates linked to the library.

What if my club isn't in the library?

Ask SCP to add it through the contact page. Adding accounts is included in every plan.
