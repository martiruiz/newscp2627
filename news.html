X 27 October 2026 Front Office Sports

World Wide Wob quits NBA X, citing algorithm shift and weak creator pay

Rob Perez, the NBA personality known as World Wide Wob, has stepped away from his X account of more than one million followers after the Knicks won the title in June. In an interview with Front Office Sports, he pointed to X's move toward a For You recommendation feed, turnover in the platform's sports team, and creator earnings he called uncompetitive versus other platforms, alongside roughly 15 years of nonstop content burnout.

Perez has not disappeared entirely. He streams LA police chases daily on Twitch, posts some NBA content on Threads, where he says he has enjoyed working with the platform's team, and keeps a protected secondary X account for his core community. He compared that approach to YouTubers running alternate channels for content outside their main focus, and did not rule out returning to X someday.

AI 27 October 2026 Front Office Sports

Sports organizations expand AI content production despite growing fan backlash

A new survey by Front Office Sports and Teamworks of more than 200 sports industry leaders found that 40% of organizations already use AI for content or media production, with another 24% planning to start within a year. Adoption is higher among pro teams (45%) than in college sports (29%), and around nine in ten respondents expect AI use to grow over the next two to three years.

The push comes amid visible fan resistance. Caitlin Clark criticized the Indiana Fever for an AI-altered Instagram image, Washington State faced criticism over an AI web-content deal with FanWord, and AI graphics at Dodger Stadium and Citi Field drew complaints. Meanwhile, the New Orleans Saints and St. Louis Blues publicly promised their graphics are made by people, not AI.

Chinese sponsors drop Mbappé after resurfaced Mao 'dictator' meme comment

INSTAGRAM 27 October 2026 Social Media Today

Australia weighs algorithm opt-out rule as Instagram's Mosseri warns of shrinking reach

Australia's government has started early consultation on rules that would require social platforms to offer a switch to turn off algorithmic recommendations, with a non-algorithmic, following-only feed as the default. Communications Minister Annika Wells argues engagement-driven feeds reward attention over well-being, and says platforms must respect a user's choice rather than burying it. Nothing has been formally tabled yet, and the proposal is still open for community input.

Instagram chief Adam Mosseri has pushed back, saying on Threads that a following-only feed would mean a smaller Instagram and less reach for everyone, including creators. Separately, commentator Michael Shellenberger has framed the plan as a speech-control measure, though key provisions he criticised have been removed or are being revised during consultation.

SPORTS MARKETING 26 October 2026 Social Media Today

Pew survey: 58% of US adults now rely on social media for news

A Pew Research report released on Oct. 23, based on a June survey of more than 3,500 US adults, found that 58% consider social media an important way to follow current events. Another 13% name social apps as their single most important source of information. In Pew's 2022 edition of the same study, those figures were 43% and 5%.

For sports social teams, this confirms that club and league accounts increasingly act as primary newsrooms rather than secondary channels. Many fans will first hear about signings, injuries, lineups or ticket news in their feeds, not on a website or through traditional media. Official accounts should prioritize speed, accuracy and clear, native-format announcements so they become the trusted first source.

INSTAGRAM 26 October 2026 Social Media Today

Instagram tests 90-minute Features videos, Clips as Reels and Series for creators

Instagram has started testing a set of long-form video tools. Features lets selected creators publish videos of up to 90 minutes, suited to podcasts, vlogs and interviews, and is expanding from Brazil to select US creators from October 26. Clips as Reels lets creators link up to eight Reels back to a longer video, while Series groups episodic Reels under a single theme. Phone-as-remote control is also coming to Instagram's connected TV app.

For sports teams, this opens the door to hosting full press conferences, behind-the-scenes documentaries, club podcasts or extended player interviews directly on Instagram, where fan communities already live. Clips as Reels is a natural fit for cutting short highlights that funnel fans into a full matchday film, and Series can package recurring formats like weekly training diaries or a season-long docuseries.

X 25 October 2026 Social Media Today

X shares holiday ad guidance: short copy, natural tone and one clear call to action

X hosted a webinar for marketers planning holiday campaigns, sharing audience insights and creative best practices. The platform says its top-performing ads run just 50 to 100 characters, roughly one sentence. It urges brands to sound like real people rather than overselling, to give users a reason to act now through limited-time or exclusive offers, and to finish with a single, clear call to action.

X also advised removing anything that distracts from the ad's goal: send users to one destination, avoid tagging other accounts, and skip hashtags and multiple emojis, which are not allowed in ads. On AI-driven shopping, it recommended describing plainly what a product does, who it is for and why it is better, so recommendation models can summarise it easily.

TIKTOK 25 October 2026 Social Media Today

TikTok US removes 14.6 million fake accounts and 781 million fake followers in Q2

TikTok USDS, the US-based group running the app's American operations since January, released its second enforcement report, covering April to June. It removed 14.6 million fake accounts, blocked 64.8 million spam accounts at sign-up, stripped 781.4 million fake followers and stopped 403.2 million fake follow requests, a clear jump on the first quarter. Video removals and livestream warnings or demonetizations also rose.

Most video takedowns fell under regulated goods and services, a broad category covering gambling, alcohol, counterfeit products and services selling fake likes, views and followers. TikTok also said it disrupted 30 covert networks trying to manipulate engagement, removing more than 76,000 accounts and 4.8 million posts, as it ramps up integrity efforts ahead of the US midterms.

TIKTOK 25 October 2026 Social Media Today

TikTok US removes over 14 million fake accounts in second-quarter enforcement report

TikTok has released account enforcement data for its US operation covering the second quarter. The platform says it removed more than 14 million fake accounts during the period. It also says it blocked close to 65 million spam accounts before they could be created, stopping them at the sign-up stage.

For sports social teams, large fake-account purges can cause sudden dips in follower counts and engagement figures. Clubs, leagues and athletes should expect occasional drops and avoid treating them as a loss of real fans. Benchmarking against engaged metrics such as watch time, shares and comments gives a more reliable picture than raw follower totals.
