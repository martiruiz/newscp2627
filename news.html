X 28 October 2026 Social Media Today

X begins sharing creator credit when original images are reposted by others

X has updated its Original Content Rewards Program so that credit for reposted images can be shared. According to the report, the change is designed to let more users benefit when engagement builds around linked graphics, rather than rewards flowing only to the account that reposted an image. The short announcement offers few further details on how the credit split will work in practice.

For sports social teams, this matters because so much matchday content on X is visual: lineups, score graphics, player cards and fan-made edits. If original creators gain credit when their images travel, clubs and leagues that produce standout graphics could see more value from wide resharing, and fan artists may be more willing to collaborate.

SPORTS MARKETING 28 October 2026 Front Office Sports

Women's sports bars push back on WNBA's 12:30 p.m. Finals Game 6 tip-off

The WNBA moved Game 6 of its Finals from 8 p.m. to 12:30 p.m. ET on a Thursday so the Atlanta Dream could play at the 17,000-seat State Farm Arena instead of the 3,500-seat Gateway Center. With Golden State up 3-2, a potential title clincher now starts at 9:30 a.m. in San Francisco. Owners of women's sports bars in Chicago, Brooklyn, Portland and Seattle told Front Office Sports the slot hurts fans and their revenue.

Several owners voiced frustration publicly, starting with an Instagram post from Level Sporting Club's founder. The Sports Bra's founder warned that weaker viewing numbers could be misread as proof the audience is missing. Seattle's Rough & Tumble said it may not open, risking roughly $10,000 to $15,000 per Finals night. Level Sporting Club chose to open anyway, promoting the game on social as a remote-work gathering, a first weekday morning opening for the bar.

INSTAGRAM 27 October 2026 Social Media Today

Meta's Edits app adds Halloween effects, beat-synced animations and bulk caption editing

Meta has rolled out a new batch of updates to Edits, its video editing app tied to Instagram. The release includes Halloween-themed fonts, sound effects and animations, 13 new text animations, and beat-synced effects such as flashing lights and zooms that automatically match a clip's music. Caption tools gain extra styles and bulk editing, while new automated color correction and per-clip volume boosts up to 150% round out the update.

Edits' carousel editor, launched on iOS earlier this month, is now also available on Android, and a new filter was created with Nigerian musician Arya Starr. Meta says a desktop version is coming soon, which would move the app closer to being a full production studio rather than a quick mobile editor.

YOUTUBE 27 October 2026 Social Media Today

YouTube adds members-only microdrama episodes as format passes 6.5 billion views

YouTube is putting more weight behind microdramas after the short serialized format topped 6.5 billion views in the first half of 2026, up more than 50% year over year. The platform is revamping members-only episodes on mobile so viewers can join a channel membership and keep watching without leaving the content, building on its recent Shorts Series feature that groups Shorts into seasons and episodes.

YouTube also highlighted auto-translation, multi-language audio and AI-generated local subtitles as tools for reaching new markets without dubbing costs, and noted that 40% of views come more than 30 days after release. Its tips for success include emotional hooks in the first 15 seconds, vertical-first framing, frequent publishing, everyday story arcs and interactive plots shaped by audience feedback.

FACEBOOK 27 October 2026 Social Media Today

Meta Ads Manager adds Breakdown tool showing performance of individual ad creative elements

Meta has introduced a Breakdown option in Ads Manager that reports how each component of an ad performs. Advertisers can see results for individual images and videos delivered as single media, as well as for each text variation. There are limits: ads using multi-post displays such as collection or carousel formats are excluded, and some custom conversion actions like Donate and Subscribe are not covered.

For club and league marketing teams running paid campaigns for ticketing, memberships or merchandise, this makes creative testing far more precise. Instead of judging an ad as a whole, teams can identify whether a matchday highlight clip outperforms a player portrait, or which caption angle drives more action, and feed those learnings into both paid and organic content planning.

LINKEDIN 27 October 2026 Social Media Today

LinkedIn tests simplified navigation and longer text previews in the feed

LinkedIn is experimenting with a streamlined navigation layout on desktop and mobile. On desktop, menus are being merged into fewer tabs: the Profile tab now gathers analytics, saved posts and settings, while a new More tab houses puzzle games, news, groups, events, newsletters and video. Mobile users will see similar consolidation in the bottom bar. The test is rolling out gradually and varies by region and language.

LinkedIn is also displaying more of each text post in the main feed before readers need to tap the more link. That could lift engagement for creators, though it weakens the old tactic of hiding the main message behind the cut-off with line-by-line posts.

YOUTUBE 27 October 2026 Social Media Today

YouTube tests $20 payout threshold for Partner Program creators, down from $100

YouTube is running an experiment that lets some Partner Program creators withdraw earnings once they reach $20, instead of the long-standing $100 minimum. Starting with October 2026 payments, a randomly chosen group of creators in the U.S., India and Indonesia is included. Unpaid balances roll over to the following month, participants get a notification, and anyone preferring a higher threshold can adjust it in AdSense for YouTube.

For sports organisations, the change matters less for big club channels than for the wider ecosystem around them: smaller athletes, academy players, women's teams, fan creators and niche sports channels that earn modest amounts. Faster access to revenue makes YouTube monetisation feel more tangible for these creators, and it adds to YouTube's appeal as Instagram pushes harder into long-form video to compete for creators.

X 27 October 2026 Front Office Sports

World Wide Wob quits NBA X, citing algorithm shift and weak creator pay

Rob Perez, the NBA personality known as World Wide Wob, has stepped away from his X account of more than one million followers after the Knicks won the title in June. In an interview with Front Office Sports, he pointed to X's move toward a For You recommendation feed, turnover in the platform's sports team, and creator earnings he called uncompetitive versus other platforms, alongside roughly 15 years of nonstop content burnout.

Perez has not disappeared entirely. He streams LA police chases daily on Twitch, posts some NBA content on Threads, where he says he has enjoyed working with the platform's team, and keeps a protected secondary X account for his core community. He compared that approach to YouTubers running alternate channels for content outside their main focus, and did not rule out returning to X someday.

AI 27 October 2026 Front Office Sports

Sports organizations expand AI content production despite growing fan backlash

A new survey by Front Office Sports and Teamworks of more than 200 sports industry leaders found that 40% of organizations already use AI for content or media production, with another 24% planning to start within a year. Adoption is higher among pro teams (45%) than in college sports (29%), and around nine in ten respondents expect AI use to grow over the next two to three years.

The push comes amid visible fan resistance. Caitlin Clark criticized the Indiana Fever for an AI-altered Instagram image, Washington State faced criticism over an AI web-content deal with FanWord, and AI graphics at Dodger Stadium and Citi Field drew complaints. Meanwhile, the New Orleans Saints and St. Louis Blues publicly promised their graphics are made by people, not AI.

SPORTS MARKETING 27 October 2026 Front Office Sports

Chinese sponsors drop Mbappé after resurfaced Mao 'dictator' meme comment

Kylian Mbappé has apologized after an old clip resurfaced online in which, asked on French show The Bridge about his favorite meme of himself, he described a face-swap with Mao Zedong as showing a Chinese dictator. Beverage brand Dongpeng, dairy company Mengniu and cheese maker Milkground ended their sponsorship deals, while Chinese state TV pulled LaLiga broadcasts and Mbappé ads. LaLiga president Javier Tebas posted an apology to China on X.

For sports social teams, the episode shows how a light-hearted podcast or show moment can resurface and trigger commercial fallout across markets. It echoes the NBA's 2019 Hong Kong tweet crisis and Brighton's apology over a social post last year. Clubs and athletes with Asian partners face real sponsorship and broadcast risk from content that feels harmless in a home market.

INSTAGRAM 27 October 2026 Social Media Today

Australia weighs algorithm opt-out rule as Instagram's Mosseri warns of shrinking reach

Australia's government has started early consultation on rules that would require social platforms to offer a switch to turn off algorithmic recommendations, with a non-algorithmic, following-only feed as the default. Communications Minister Annika Wells argues engagement-driven feeds reward attention over well-being, and says platforms must respect a user's choice rather than burying it. Nothing has been formally tabled yet, and the proposal is still open for community input.

Instagram chief Adam Mosseri has pushed back, saying on Threads that a following-only feed would mean a smaller Instagram and less reach for everyone, including creators. Separately, commentator Michael Shellenberger has framed the plan as a speech-control measure, though key provisions he criticised have been removed or are being revised during consultation.
