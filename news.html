X 10 November 2026 Social Media Today

X launches video clipping so users can reshare credited snippets of longer videos

X has introduced a clipping option for videos lasting two minutes or more. From the repost menu, users can select a segment between 5 and 60 seconds, capped at 30% of the original, and share it with their own commentary. The clip carries an overlay with the creator's handle, a timestamp showing where it sits in the full video, and a tap-through link to the original.

Both the original poster and the account sharing the clip are credited with resulting impressions, so each can earn through X's Original Creator Rewards program. Creators currently cannot disable clipping; the only way to remove unwanted clips is deleting the original post. Live broadcasts are not yet supported. The feature is live on iOS and Android, with web access to follow.

YOUTUBE 10 November 2026 Social Media Today

YouTube survey of 20,000 EU creators highlights demand for studios, support and algorithm transparency

YouTube has released video interviews with European creators that expand on its recent creator consultation, which surveyed 20,000 creators across all 27 EU member states. The initial findings, published in a 17-page Creative Capital report, list five main concerns: more government recognition and support, closer collaboration with traditional industries, more investment in skills training, better access to physical production space such as studios, and clearer information on how recommendation algorithms affect reach.

YouTube is using the research to engage policymakers. It says it contributed more than $9.3 billion to the European economy in 2025 and supported over 220,000 jobs. For sports organisations, the findings show where creators feel underserved, and those gaps line up with assets many clubs and leagues already have: production facilities, access, and an established traditional media business.

TIKTOK 9 November 2026 Social Media Today

TikTok upgrades Symphony AI ad tools with latest Dreamina Seedance video model

TikTok has expanded its Symphony AI ad creation suite. The Symphony Agent now taps the newest Dreamina Seedance models, letting advertisers upload up to 50 multimodal references (images, video and audio) instead of 9, and generate clips of up to 30 seconds per prompt, double the earlier 15-second cap. TikTok says output is sharper, with better lighting and more consistent characters across frames.

TikTok also released an updated Symphony API so advertisers can run Symphony generation inside their own creative management tools. In addition, starting November 9, advertisers outside North America can buy BytePlus tokens to access Seedance through Symphony. BytePlus is the enterprise tech arm of parent company ByteDance and runs its AI systems.

LINKEDIN 9 November 2026 Social Media Today

LinkedIn details AI Profile Relevance model shaping which non-connection posts users see

LinkedIn has explained a new Profile Relevance model that decides which posts from people outside a member's network appear in their feed. The system uses LLM-generated summaries of each member's profile and posting history, then scores posts against factors such as topic, location, job title, company, industry, seniority and education. In tests, LinkedIn reported a 0.54% lift in feed updates viewed, a 0.94% rise in viral rate and an 18.8% drop in hide rate.

For sports organisations, this means LinkedIn reach beyond followers increasingly depends on how clearly a post signals its professional context. Clubs and leagues using the platform for sponsorship, partnerships, hiring or business storytelling should be explicit about topic, industry and location, so the model can match content to sponsors, agencies, media buyers and sports-business professionals who don't yet follow them.

SPORTS MARKETING 9 November 2026 SponCon Sports

Steelers and Snickers turn bye week into 8.7M-view sponsored challenge video

The Pittsburgh Steelers used their bye week, which falls at the midpoint of the NFL season, to publish a sponsored video with Snickers. Players took on a popular social challenge: cutting a Snickers bar into two halves of equal weight. The post tied the idea to splitting the season in half and closed by urging fans to satisfy their halftime hunger. Across channels, the content passed 8.7 million views.

For sports social teams, the lesson is that a familiar trend becomes premium sponsor inventory when timing, wordplay and the product all line up. Calendar moments such as bye weeks, the halfway point of a season or international breaks are often quiet content windows. Building a partner concept around them can fill the gap with something fans want to watch rather than a straightforward advert.

AI 8 November 2026 Social Media Today

Meta's Muse AI agent can now build custom mini apps called Spaces

Meta has added Spaces to its Muse AI agent, letting users turn a request into a dedicated mini app stored in the Muse sidebar for ongoing tracking. Meta's examples include family activity logs, package trackers and product recommendations drawn from Instagram activity. Muse and Meta AI also gain Active Memory, which recalls relevant details from past chats, and Active Focus, which helps agents keep working on a task when obstacles appear.

For sports social teams, Spaces could act as lightweight dashboards built without developers: a matchday content checklist, a tracker for sponsor deliverables, or a running log of athlete content requests. Expanded memory means the assistant can retain context such as brand tone, posting cadence or campaign details across conversations, reducing repetitive briefing when drafting ideas or planning schedules.

SPORTS MARKETING 5 November 2026 SponCon Sports

MLB postseason lifts social engagement per post by 51%, reshaping sponsorship pricing

An analysis by SponCon Sports and Mondo Metrics of 57,239 posts from all 12 MLB postseason clubs found engagements per post rose 51% and views per post 26% versus the regular season. Even after adjusting for teams shifting more content to Instagram and TikTok, engagement still climbed 32%. Facebook showed the largest jump, with engagements up 79%, while World Series clubs averaged a 168% lift compared with 13% for Wild Card exits.

The takeaway for partnership and social teams is that playoff inventory deserves its own pricing, but not a blanket markup. The author recommends each club calculate its own postseason bump by platform and, ideally, by content series, then refresh those benchmarks every year it qualifies, since algorithms and fan behavior shift. Teams without recent playoff data can use the MLB findings as a starting reference.

Study finds citing sources in YouTube descriptions boosts trust and sharing

A new study shows that YouTube creators who list their sources in video descriptions earn more viewer trust and make audiences more willing to share their content. It also found that adopting newsroom habits, such as citing, adding context and correcting errors, improves how trustworthy news videos are perceived, a lesson that extends beyond journalists to brands and creators.

YOUTUBE 5 November 2026 SocialGeek News

YouTube tests lowering creator payout threshold from 100 to 20 dollars

YouTube is testing a reduction of the minimum earnings creators must reach before getting paid, cutting it from 100 to 20 dollars. The change would help smaller channels that currently wait months to hit the threshold and receive any income, and is read as an effort to attract and keep emerging creators as Instagram moves into long-form video.

For sports organizations, faster payouts could encourage more niche fan creators, ex-players and grassroots channels to professionalize. That widens the pool of smaller, highly engaged creators that clubs and sponsors can partner with for targeted campaigns, such as academy content, women's teams coverage or local fan-led shows, often at lower cost than big names.

INSTAGRAM 5 November 2026 SocialGeek News

Instagram tests Features, horizontal videos up to 90 minutes linked from Reels

Instagram is trialling a format called Features that lets creators publish horizontal videos of up to 90 minutes, aimed at podcasts, interviews and entertainment shows. Reels can link straight to the full episode, and a Clips as Reels option cuts segments from a Feature into Reels. Features can surface in Explore and recommendations, and Instagram wants to bring them to TV screens.

The test is limited to a group of US creators after an earlier trial in Brazil. Monetization remains unclear: unlike YouTube, Instagram has not announced any ad revenue sharing for the format yet, and the move is widely seen as a direct challenge to YouTube's long-form dominance.

SNAPCHAT 5 November 2026 Social Media Today

Snapchat user growth stalls in North America and Europe while Spotlight surges

Snap's Q3 2026 results show Snapchat reaching 495 million daily active users, just 2 million more than the previous quarter, with zero growth in North America and Europe. Revenue still rose 14% year over year to $1.72 billion. Teen social media bans are a growing risk: in Australia, Snapchat locked or disabled more than 415,000 accounts belonging to under-16 users.

The bright spot is short-form video. Snap says the number of people posting to Spotlight grew 130% year over year and daily active viewers rose 40%, while messaging and two-way communication also climbed. Subscriptions across Snapchat+, Lens+ and Memories Storage passed 30 million, and Snap is expanding creator partnership options for brands.
