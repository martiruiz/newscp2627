SPORTS MARKETING 19 November 2026 SponCon Sports

Why sports organizations should align content and partnerships teams to grow sponsorship revenue

In his Sponcon Sports newsletter, consultant Alex Kopilow argues that the most neglected relationship in sports organizations is the one between content and partnerships teams. Drawing on his consulting work and time at the Chicago White Sox, he says sellers often pitch formats content teams cannot deliver, while partnerships staff miss sellable ideas because they never hear what content is planning.

He proposes six changes for 2027: cross-attending each other's weekly meetings, bringing content staff into pitches and discovery sessions, building a shared digital inventory, sharing pitch updates and deal values, educating each other on goals and metrics, and giving the wider marketing department a shared digital sponsorship revenue target with a team bonus, provided audience and content standards are maintained.

YOUTUBE 19 November 2026 SocialGeek News

YouTube public view counts now estimate people watching together on TV

YouTube's public counters now include an estimate of people watching a video together on a TV screen, covering long-form videos, Shorts and live streams. Previously accumulated totals are not being recalculated, and creator payouts are unchanged by the update.

Sports content is often watched in groups, from highlights on the living-room TV to live shows and watch-alongs, so clubs and leagues may see visible view numbers rise on connected-TV content. Social teams should note the change date when comparing performance over time and when reporting reach to sponsors, since older figures will not be adjusted.

INSTAGRAM 19 November 2026 SocialGeek News

Instagram lets creators tag Amazon affiliate products directly in Reels and feed posts

Instagram now allows creators to tag Amazon products with affiliate links inside their content, up to 30 products per Reel and 20 per feed post. Once a creator connects an Amazon account through the Professional Dashboard, affiliate attribution is added automatically. Instagram takes no cut; Amazon continues paying creators for qualifying purchases.

For sports, this cuts the friction of the old 'link in bio' approach. Athletes can tag the boots, recovery gear or training equipment they actually use in a workout Reel, and fans can buy without leaving the video. Recommendation content becomes a direct revenue line for athletes and creators.

SPORTS MARKETING 19 November 2026 SocialGeek News

Twitch brings back Hosting and tests shoppable ads at TwitchCon

At TwitchCon, Twitch announced that Hosting is returning, with tests starting at the end of the year: an offline channel can show another live stream with shared chat, and 'Host Trains' rotate audiences across chosen channels. Streamers will also be able to invite up to seven viewers into Luna games using their phones as controllers, and a new apprentice moderator role can time out users but not ban them.

On the business side, shoppable ads are in desktop beta for US and Canadian advertisers, letting viewers add products to a cart mid-stream. A skippable ad format enters open beta in December, with international shopping planned for early 2027. Twitch says creators landing sponsorships have quadrupled in a year, while total payouts have doubled.

TIKTOK 18 November 2026 Social Media Today

TikTok Music Impact Report links viral moments to 94% of Billboard Global 200 hits

TikTok has released its 2026 Music Impact Report, built on a Luminate study of more than 4,000 U.S. users. It finds 45% of U.S. TikTok users discover new music in the app, ahead of YouTube, Instagram and Facebook. The headline claim: 94% of songs entering the Billboard Global 200 in 2025 went viral on TikTok first, up from 84% the year before.

The report also says songs with a viral TikTok moment stay on the chart four times longer, and that U.S. music listeners on TikTok are 78% more likely than the general population to pay for streaming. Most emerging artists surveyed see a clear link between TikTok engagement and activity on streaming services.

LINKEDIN 18 November 2026 Social Media Today

LinkedIn launches Sumbreak, its ninth in-app puzzle game, to boost daily engagement

LinkedIn has released Sumbreak, a math puzzle in which players merge matching numbered tiles that add together until only one tile remains. It is the platform's ninth in-stream game. Members can challenge others, leaderboards now support favorites for comparing scores with chosen connections, bonus puzzles reward daily returners, and games are easier to find in the updated interface.

LinkedIn says billions of puzzles have been solved since games arrived in May 2024. In March 2025 it reported millions of professionals playing daily, with 84% of players returning the next day. The aim is clear: habitual daily visits and more conversation, driven by friendly rivalry rather than purely professional networking.

Fox's Big Noon Kickoff Heads to Division II Ferris State, Sparking Social Buzz

Fox Sports' Big Noon Kickoff will broadcast from Division II Ferris State in Big Rapids, Michigan, ahead of the Bulldogs' D-II playoff opener against Truman State. INSTAGRAM 16 November 2026 Social Media Today

Instagram launches dedicated app for Apple's foldable iPhone Duo with side-by-side Reels

Instagram has released a version of its app built specifically for the iPhone Duo, using the device's larger unfolded display. The main feed opens in a wider format, Reels show the video and its comments next to each other while playback continues, control buttons move to a sidebar, and messages open in a two-column layout. When the phone is folded, Stories and Reels keep their usual vertical format.

For sports social teams, the side-by-side Reels view puts the comment section on equal footing with the clip itself. That makes it worth seeding conversation early: pinned replies from the club account, prompts asking fans to predict a result or rate a goal, and quick responses to top comments can all become part of the viewing experience rather than an afterthought.

X 16 November 2026 Social Media Today

X tests work experience section that lets users list career history on profiles

X is experimenting with a work experience element on user profiles, letting people add their professional history, according to images shared by app researcher Radu Oncescu. X has not officially announced the feature. It echoes the platform's earlier job push: job listings for verified organizations launched in August 2023, and X reported more than 750,000 open roles by June 2024, though its hiring profile and job search have since gone dormant.

The test may signal a renewed attempt to compete with LinkedIn and reconnect job seekers with brands. A more skeptical reading is that X wants richer professional data, potentially feeding audience insights into xAI. Either way, profiles on X could soon carry more career context, changing how accounts present themselves and how users judge credibility.
