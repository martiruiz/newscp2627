How Barcelona, Liverpool and PSG Turn Google Gemini Deals Into Useful Fan Content

FC Barcelona is running a pre-match Instagram Story series sponsored by Google Gemini. A four-frame sequence opens with a mock Gemini prompt asking for facts about the next opponent, followed by three frames of answers paired with player imagery. The series is part of the Gemini and Pixel partnerships Google announced in August with Barcelona, PSG, Arsenal, Liverpool and Bayern Munich. A SponCon Sports analysis praises it for showing a practical way to use the product rather than simply displaying AI output.

The analysis also notes limits. The slides are text-heavy, and PSG's similar feed carousels averaged 30.8K engagements across three posts reviewed, modest for a club with more than 60M followers. Other activations have done more. Liverpool used Gemini to introduce new signing Bradley Barcola and to quiz fans, and recalled a win over Brentford with linked YouTube clips. Barcelona's October fixture preview passed 482K likes, and its players now ask Gemini a question during YouTube membership Q&As.

TIKTOK 22 October 2026 SocialGeek News

TikTok lets users repost individual comments, turning replies into standalone content

TikTok is rolling out a feature that allows people to repost a single comment without sharing the original video. Funny, useful or interesting comments can now earn reach on their own, and replies can also be discovered outside the thread where they first appeared. The comment section effectively becomes a content format in its own right rather than a secondary space beneath the video.

For sports social teams, this makes community management a direct growth lever. A sharp reply from the club account, a witty reaction from a player, or a standout fan comment after a big result can now travel independently and reach audiences who never saw the original clip. That raises the value of having fast, on-brand responders ready during matches and key moments.

Louis Vuitton Adds Victory Patches to F1 Trophy Trunk After Every Grand Prix

Louis Vuitton is extending its Formula 1 trophy-trunk partnership with a new ritual: after each Grand Prix, a Victory Patch honouring the winning driver is applied to the trunk. The moment is shared through split-screen videos that pair the patch application with race highlights and podium celebrations, turning the luxury brand's product into part of the story of each win.

For sports social teams, the lesson is to build sponsored content around a repeatable ritual tied to real results. Similar to Red Bull Racing's race-specific stickers with YETI, a recurring format gives fans a reason to follow along all season and gives partners guaranteed, emotionally charged exposure. Clubs could adapt this with trophies, kit details or locker-room items updated after every victory.

SPORTS MARKETING 15 October 2026 SponCon Sports

Bryson DeChambeau's Backyard Games with Golf Clash draws 83.5M views across five platforms

Bryson DeChambeau partnered with EA's Golf Clash on Backyard Games, a daily series in which 10 golf creators competed on a three-hole course he built at home, with the winner facing him for $100,000. According to Mondo Metrics data cited by SponCon Sports, the campaign produced 83.5 million views, 3.61 million engagements and a 4.32% engagement rate across 64 posts. A teaser carousel playing on rumours he was 'coming home' earned the most sends.

The integration stood out: branded sets and game-style graphics, six-second ad reads during natural pauses, Instagram Stories with download prompts, and an in-game recreation of the course fans could play. The format grew from an unsponsored 2024 challenge that proved audience appeal before sponsors joined. However, views across Instagram, TikTok and YouTube fell each season, though YouTube Shorts average views per post rose 41% since 2024.

FACEBOOK 15 October 2026 SocialGeek News

Facebook launches 'Your Algorithm' letting users tell the feed what they want

Facebook has introduced a feature called "Your Algorithm" that lets people directly shape what appears in their feed. Users can add and manage topics they care about, answer prompts about why they liked or disliked a recommended post, and type in their own words what they want to see more or less of. The system blends these explicit inputs with familiar signals such as likes, comments and watch time.

For sports social teams, this shifts the balance away from pure engagement bait. If a fan tells Facebook they are not interested in a certain type of content, optimising it harder will not help. Topic relevance matters more, so clubs and leagues should make sure their posts are clearly tied to recognisable themes fans would actively choose, like their team, competition, players or specific sport.

Carolina Panthers Turn Upset Win Over Lions Into Bojangles Sponsored Trash Talk

SPORTS MARKETING 8 October 2026 SponCon Sports

NHL opening week sponsored content: 618 branded posts and the activations that worked

A new edition of the Sponcon Sports NHL scouting report, built with Mondo Metrics data, reviewed 8,609 posts from all 32 teams across five platforms during the season's first week. Of those, 618 carried a brand integration, about 7.2% of team output, generating 1.2M engagements and at least 13.1M views for 125 brands. The Pittsburgh Penguins led with 155K sponsored engagements, while United Airlines topped views thanks to Chicago's Patrick Kane return series.

Standout ideas included Pittsburgh's U.S. Steel welder's helmet used as a win prop, Ottawa's First Blood promotion donating to blood services when the team scores first, and series names that naturally fit the partner, like casino-backed Winning Numbers. The report also flagged weaknesses: sponsors tagged in captions but invisible in video, and sweepstakes pushed mainly through organic posts when email, apps, SMS or paid media would convert better.

INSTAGRAM 8 October 2026 SocialGeek News

Instagram tests Reels-first home screen and a paid read-only browsing mode

Instagram is testing a redesigned home in which Reels fill the first tab when the app opens, with Stories sitting just above them. The familiar feed of followed accounts, including a chronological option, sits one swipe away, and sound plays by default. Users can switch back to the old layout in Settings. Separately, Instagram is trialling a read-only mode that blocks likes, comments, DMs, follows and Story views, reportedly tied to its paid Instagram Plus package.

For sports social teams, the Reels-first home is another signal that Meta is prioritising short video and recommended content over posts from followed accounts. Clubs that still lean on static graphics such as lineups, final scores or sponsor cards should plan vertical video versions built for discovery, with strong opening seconds and audio designed to be heard, since sound now plays automatically.

SPORTS MARKETING 1 October 2026 SponCon Sports

How Akron Football Turned a Viral Ticket Promotion Into Arby's Sponsorship Inventory

Akron football invited one season-ticket holder to help script the opening offensive series of its home opener. The August announcement drew 23 million views on X, sold out suites for the first time in program history, and attracted entries from personalities like Big Cat and Jon Gruden. After the winner was revealed, Arby's Steak Nuggets came on as presenting sponsor, timed to the product's nationwide return as a limited-time offer.

One critique: a behind-the-scenes Reel that opened at an Arby's underperformed, showing sponsor requests should not override what fans came to see.

INSTAGRAM 1 October 2026 SocialGeek News

Mosseri explains Instagram ranking signals: engagement rate, timing, originality and testing

Instagram head Adam Mosseri has released new videos breaking down how the platform decides what to show users. His key points: interaction rates matter more than raw views or likes, posting time still counts because the feed stays roughly chronological, original content is prioritised over reposts, and small accounts can be shown to non-followers to test their reach. He also noted that AI models increasingly drive ranking.

For club and league social teams, the message is to judge posts by engagement rate rather than view counts, and to schedule content around when fans are actually online, such as kick-off, half-time and full-time windows. Reposting broadcaster clips or fan videos is likely to be outranked by the original creator, so in-house footage from training, the tunnel and the dressing room should carry more weight.

SPORTS MARKETING 24 September 2026 SponCon Sports

Red Bull's Max vs. 100 karting stunt drew over 241 million social views

Red Bull's Max vs. 100 event, which put Max Verstappen at the back of a 101-kart field at Silverstone, generated at least 241.3 million views and 14.5 million engagements across 633 posts from 146 accounts, according to a Sponcon Sports analysis with Mondo Metrics. Race day alone delivered about half of all views, while Red Bull's own brand accounts produced roughly 76% of measured views.

The campaign worked as a system: regional Red Bull accounts localized the same creative, a launch-style post for Max's kart kept interest alive before race day, and participating creators told their own stories. Creator posts had a higher median engagement than brand posts. Instagram delivered both reach and engagement, TikTok drove outsized interaction, and Facebook Reels produced two of the three most-viewed posts.
