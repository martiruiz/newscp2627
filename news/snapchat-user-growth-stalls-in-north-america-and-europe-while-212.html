Snapchat user growth stalls in North America and Europe while Spotlight surges

Social News · 5 November 2026 · Source: Social Media Today

Snap's Q3 2026 results show Snapchat reaching 495 million daily active users, just 2 million more than the previous quarter, with zero growth in North America and Europe. Revenue still rose 14% year over year to $1.72 billion. Teen social media bans are a growing risk: in Australia, Snapchat locked or disabled more than 415,000 accounts belonging to under-16 users.

The bright spot is short-form video. Snap says the number of people posting to Spotlight grew 130% year over year and daily active viewers rose 40%, while messaging and two-way communication also climbed. Subscriptions across Snapchat+, Lens+ and Memories Storage passed 30 million, and Snap is expanding creator partnership options for brands.

For clubs and leagues, Snapchat remains a strong channel for reaching teen fans in Western markets, but audience growth there has plateaued and age restrictions may shrink it. Teams should prioritise Spotlight clips and AR Lenses over chasing reach, track youth-ban developments in their key markets, and consider testing creator collaborations to keep younger supporters engaged.

Dan Rapaport discusses the Internet Invitational and where YouTube golf goes next

Scratch Golf's Dan Rapaport joined Front Office Sports' David Rumsey for a video conversation on golf's evolving media landscape. They covered his role calling the Skins Game on Prime Video on Black Friday, why players have to carry a made-for-TV format, whether Tiger Woods is now a special-event golfer, and the future of the Internet Invitational and the broader YouTube golf scene.

For sports social teams, the discussion shows how creator-led competitions on YouTube now sit alongside streaming-platform specials as serious golf properties. Clubs, tours and athletes can borrow the formula: personality-driven, made-for-digital events where talent carries the entertainment. Partnering with established golf creators or building collaborative YouTube formats can reach younger fans who follow the sport through creators rather than traditional broadcasts.

YOUTUBE 24 November 2026 Front Office Sports

Kalshi sponsors 49ers WAGs' YouTube series to reach female NFL fans

Prediction market Kalshi has partnered with Wife'd Up, Mic'd Up, the YouTube series hosted by 49ers spouses Kristin Juszczyk and Claire Kittle. The six-episode deal features a guest partner of another NFL player in each installment, plus a branded prediction segment tied to her gameday. The first episode, with Haley Cavinder, passed 130,000 views, and launch videos on Instagram drew nearly 700,000 views combined.

The integration is built natively into creator formats: a get-ready-with-me announcement, onscreen QR codes, sign-up links in descriptions and segments woven into real travel and gameday stories. Kalshi says the goal is reaching new audiences, mainly women, whose presence on the platform it says grew about 60% in a year. The NFL bans players from endorsing prediction markets, raising questions since Kyle Juszczyk appears in the content.

X 23 November 2026 Front Office Sports

Kirk Herbstreit distances himself from viral SEC Shorts sketch mocking ESPN and CFP

Comedy group SEC Shorts posted a roughly two-and-a-half-minute sketch on X lampooning the College Football Playoff committee and ESPN's rankings show, including a parody of analyst Heather Dinich. The video passed 2 million views and drew thousands of replies in under nine hours. ESPN's Kirk Herbstreit appears at the end delivering an ad read for Renasant Bank, the presenting sponsor of every SEC Shorts video.

Herbstreit told Front Office Sports he works with Renasant, which uses him on SEC Shorts' weekly clips, but has no partnership with the group and no role in its content. ESPN declined to comment, while Dinich appeared to respond with a light jab promoting her upcoming book. The episode shows how a sponsor's ad placement can tie talent to content they never approved.
