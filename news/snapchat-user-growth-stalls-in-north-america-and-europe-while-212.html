Snapchat user growth stalls in North America and Europe while Spotlight surges

Social News · 5 November 2026 · Source: Social Media Today

Snap's Q3 2026 results show Snapchat reaching 495 million daily active users, just 2 million more than the previous quarter, with zero growth in North America and Europe. Revenue still rose 14% year over year to $1.72 billion. Teen social media bans are a growing risk: in Australia, Snapchat locked or disabled more than 415,000 accounts belonging to under-16 users.

The bright spot is short-form video. Snap says the number of people posting to Spotlight grew 130% year over year and daily active viewers rose 40%, while messaging and two-way communication also climbed. Subscriptions across Snapchat+, Lens+ and Memories Storage passed 30 million, and Snap is expanding creator partnership options for brands.

For clubs and leagues, Snapchat remains a strong channel for reaching teen fans in Western markets, but audience growth there has plateaued and age restrictions may shrink it. Teams should prioritise Spotlight clips and AR Lenses over chasing reach, track youth-ban developments in their key markets, and consider testing creator collaborations to keep younger supporters engaged.

NFL, Commanders and ESPN use Instagram collabs to hype Sam Hartman's Bachelor casting

Washington Commanders practice squad quarterback Sam Hartman was announced as the lead for the 30th season of ABC's The Bachelor. Right after the reveal on Good Morning America, the NFL, the Commanders and ESPN published Instagram collaboration posts with ABC and the show's official account, plus similar TikTok content. The NFL's slow-motion clip of Hartman's Combine 40-yard dash passed a million views by midday, while static photos from the team and ESPN drew over 400,000 combined likes.

The push fits a wider NFL strategy: the league previously said it is scaling up female-focused content, including training camp videos featuring quarterbacks with their partners. ESPN also plans to keep coordinating with Disney sister brands on Bachelor content relevant to its fans. Hartman's former college programs, Wake Forest and Notre Dame, joined in as well.

NBA expands creator partnership with Kenny Beecham and Enjoy Basketball

The NBA has widened its content deal with creator Kenny Beecham and his Enjoy Basketball brand. A three-episode pilot of Enjoy the NBA Trivia Show becomes an eight-episode season on NBA TV, the NBA app and Enjoy Basketball's YouTube channel. Other shows from the brand will run on league platforms, and the creators also get access to official NBA footage.

The agreement adds a live show at the NBA Crossover fan festival during All-Star weekend in Phoenix and regular guest spots for Beecham and Pierre Andresen on NBA TV's The Association. Enjoy Basketball has 142,000 YouTube subscribers, while Beecham's personal following is largest on X. He says the group will keep criticising the league when it disagrees, and that the NBA values that authenticity.

YOUTUBE 12 November 2026 SponCon Sports

Mercedes F1's Antonelli title interview shows how YouTube packaging makes or breaks sponsored content

After Kimi Antonelli became Formula 1's youngest World Champion, Mercedes released an exclusive interview on its Nu-sponsored video podcast. Despite a channel of over 2M subscribers, the 30-minute episode drew about 30K views and nine comments in six days. Its title led with the series name and episode number, and the thumbnail was crowded with show branding and Nu's logo. F1's own interview, framed clearly as the champion's first interview, reached 935K views in a week.

YouTube strategist Gen Kimura argued Mercedes did not lead with value: YouTube is intent-driven, so viewers need a reason to click. He also called subscriber counts a vanity metric, saying brands increasingly look at a channel's consistent view floor. Meanwhile, Mercedes' Instagram announcement and clips generated 2.9M views and over 727K engagements, a reminder to measure sponsor value across the whole content package.
