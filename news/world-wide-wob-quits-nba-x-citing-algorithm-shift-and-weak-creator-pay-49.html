World Wide Wob quits NBA X, citing algorithm shift and weak creator pay

Social News · 27 October 2026 · Source: Front Office Sports

Rob Perez, the NBA personality known as World Wide Wob, has stepped away from his X account of more than one million followers after the Knicks won the title in June. In an interview with Front Office Sports, he pointed to X's move toward a For You recommendation feed, turnover in the platform's sports team, and creator earnings he called uncompetitive versus other platforms, alongside roughly 15 years of nonstop content burnout.

Perez has not disappeared entirely. He streams LA police chases daily on Twitch, posts some NBA content on Threads, where he says he has enjoyed working with the platform's team, and keeps a protected secondary X account for his core community. He compared that approach to YouTubers running alternate channels for content outside their main focus, and did not rule out returning to X someday.

For sports social teams, this is a signal worth tracking: prominent basketball voices are reassessing X as an algorithm-driven feed weakens follower-based reach. Clubs and leagues should audit how much of their engagement depends on X, test Threads and live streaming, and maintain direct relationships with platform partnership teams. When working with creators, consider that many now spread their presence across platforms and secondary accounts, so partnerships should follow the audience, not a single app.

X 28 October 2026 Social Media Today

X begins sharing creator credit when original images are reposted by others

X has updated its Original Content Rewards Program so that credit for reposted images can be shared. According to the report, the change is designed to let more users benefit when engagement builds around linked graphics, rather than rewards flowing only to the account that reposted an image. The short announcement offers few further details on how the credit split will work in practice.

For sports social teams, this matters because so much matchday content on X is visual: lineups, score graphics, player cards and fan-made edits. If original creators gain credit when their images travel, clubs and leagues that produce standout graphics could see more value from wide resharing, and fan artists may be more willing to collaborate.

X 25 October 2026 Social Media Today

X shares holiday ad guidance: short copy, natural tone and one clear call to action

X hosted a webinar for marketers planning holiday campaigns, sharing audience insights and creative best practices. The platform says its top-performing ads run just 50 to 100 characters, roughly one sentence. It urges brands to sound like real people rather than overselling, to give users a reason to act now through limited-time or exclusive offers, and to finish with a single, clear call to action.

X also advised removing anything that distracts from the ad's goal: send users to one destination, avoid tagging other accounts, and skip hashtags and multiple emojis, which are not allowed in ads. On AI-driven shopping, it recommended describing plainly what a product does, who it is for and why it is better, so recommendation models can summarise it easily.

SPORTS MARKETING 29 October 2026 Front Office Sports

PGA Tour Lands Short-Term Sponsor for Austin Event After Good Good Golf Exit

The PGA Tour has found a partial replacement for Good Good Golf, the creator group that withdrew from sponsoring and hosting a new Austin tournament after backlash over a controversial Callaway driver ad. Compliance Solutions, a Florida tax services firm already backing a Korn Ferry Tour event, will become presenting sponsor of the renamed Austin Championship for this year only, paying less than the $6 million Good Good had committed.

The reduced fee reflects how little time remains to activate: practice rounds start in about 11 days, leaving the sponsor almost no runway for promotion. Meanwhile, Good Good has also lost its Callaway deal, its Golf Channel show and retail placement for its merchandise, showing how quickly a creator brand's commercial ecosystem can unravel after a misstep.
