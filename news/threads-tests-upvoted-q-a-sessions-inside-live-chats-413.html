Threads tests upvoted Q&A sessions inside live chats

Social News · 19 November 2026 · Source: Social Media Today

Threads is trialling a Q&A format within its live chats. Hosts can collect questions from participants, and the chat then votes on which ones should be asked, creating a more structured, interview-style session. The approach resembles Reddit's AMA model, where the community steers the conversation by upvoting. The test is running in a limited number of live chat sessions, though every participant can read the answers.

The feature builds on live chat, which Threads launched in April and expanded in September, as Meta pushes the app toward real-time conversation around major events. X still leads for live event discussion, but Threads is gaining ground and wants to be a stronger option for communities and brands looking to engage audiences in the moment.

For sports teams, voted Q&As are a natural fit for player takeovers, coach press-conference previews, or post-match debriefs, letting fans surface the questions they care about most. Sponsors could back a recurring fan Q&A series, and clubs can mine the top-voted questions for content ideas. If the feature rolls out widely, it offers a low-effort way to build matchday community on Threads.

THREADS 29 October 2026 Social Media Today

Threads tests Threads Friends, a Close Friends-style list for private posting

Threads is trialling Threads Friends, a feature that lets users build a private list of up to 50 connections and publish posts visible only to that group. It works much like Instagram's Close Friends: people added can see they are on the list, and members can view each other's likes and replies on those posts. The test is running in Ireland, Italy and Japan, though anyone worldwide can be added to a list.

For sports social teams, this points to a broader shift from public broadcasting toward smaller, curated sharing spaces. Athletes could use a Friends list to share behind-the-scenes thoughts with family, teammates or a handful of trusted creators, while clubs might reserve it for media partners, ambassadors or a select group of superfans.

X 23 November 2026 Front Office Sports

Kirk Herbstreit distances himself from viral SEC Shorts sketch mocking ESPN and CFP

Comedy group SEC Shorts posted a roughly two-and-a-half-minute sketch on X lampooning the College Football Playoff committee and ESPN's rankings show, including a parody of analyst Heather Dinich. The video passed 2 million views and drew thousands of replies in under nine hours. ESPN's Kirk Herbstreit appears at the end delivering an ad read for Renasant Bank, the presenting sponsor of every SEC Shorts video.

Herbstreit told Front Office Sports he works with Renasant, which uses him on SEC Shorts' weekly clips, but has no partnership with the group and no role in its content. ESPN declined to comment, while Dinich appeared to respond with a light jab promoting her upcoming book. The episode shows how a sponsor's ad placement can tie talent to content they never approved.

SPORTS MARKETING 23 November 2026 SponCon Sports

Dr Pepper reaches college football fans through House of Highlights' CLASH debate series

Dr Pepper has found a way into college football conversations without partnering with a team or league. The brand worked with CLASH, the debate and trivia channel from House of Highlights, on three episodes featuring coach Ed Orgeron, creator MMG and former Heisman winner Troy Smith. Alongside product placement and branded messaging, the activation added merchandise QR codes and paid ads running against the videos, pushing views past 2 million.

The lesson for sports marketers is that fan attention increasingly sits with media brands and creators, not only official rights holders. Brands can build relevance in a sport by sponsoring the shows and debate formats fans already watch, then layering commerce hooks such as QR codes and paid amplification to extend reach and measure response.
