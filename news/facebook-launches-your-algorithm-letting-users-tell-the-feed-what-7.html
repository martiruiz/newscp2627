Facebook launches 'Your Algorithm' letting users tell the feed what they want

Social News · 15 October 2026 · Source: SocialGeek News

Key takeaways

  • Facebook's new 'Your Algorithm' feature lets users add and manage topics, explain why they liked or disliked a post, and describe in their own words what they want to see more or less of.
  • The feed blends these explicit user inputs with familiar signals such as likes, comments and watch time.
  • For sports social teams, engagement bait loses power: if a fan says they are not interested in a type of content, optimising it harder will not help.
  • Clubs and leagues should tie posts to recognisable themes fans would actively choose, like their team, competition, players or sport, using consistent naming and clear captions.
  • Teams can build content pillars such as matchday coverage, behind-the-scenes and youth academy stories, and encourage supporters to add the club as a followed interest.

Facebook has introduced a feature called "Your Algorithm" that lets people directly shape what appears in their feed. Users can add and manage topics they care about, answer prompts about why they liked or disliked a recommended post, and type in their own words what they want to see more or less of. The system blends these explicit inputs with familiar signals such as likes, comments and watch time.

For sports social teams, this shifts the balance away from pure engagement bait. If a fan tells Facebook they are not interested in a certain type of content, optimising it harder will not help. Topic relevance matters more, so clubs and leagues should make sure their posts are clearly tied to recognisable themes fans would actively choose, like their team, competition, players or specific sport.

In practice, that means consistent naming, clear captions and content pillars that map to topics fans might select, such as matchday coverage, behind-the-scenes or youth academy stories. Teams could also encourage supporters to add the club as a followed interest, and should watch for changes in reach on content that relies on curiosity hooks rather than genuine fan interest.

Meta Ads Manager adds Breakdown tool showing performance of individual ad creative elements

Meta has introduced a Breakdown option in Ads Manager that reports how each component of an ad performs. Advertisers can see results for individual images and videos delivered as single media, as well as for each text variation. There are limits: ads using multi-post displays such as collection or carousel formats are excluded, and some custom conversion actions like Donate and Subscribe are not covered.

For club and league marketing teams running paid campaigns for ticketing, memberships or merchandise, this makes creative testing far more precise. Instead of judging an ad as a whole, teams can identify whether a matchday highlight clip outperforms a player portrait, or which caption angle drives more action, and feed those learnings into both paid and organic content planning.

X begins sharing creator credit when original images are reposted by others

X has updated its Original Content Rewards Program so that credit for reposted images can be shared.
