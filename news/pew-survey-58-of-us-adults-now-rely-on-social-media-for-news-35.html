Pew survey: 58% of US adults now rely on social media for news

Social News · 26 October 2026 · Source: Social Media Today

A Pew Research report released on Oct. 23, based on a June survey of more than 3,500 US adults, found that 58% consider social media an important way to follow current events. Another 13% name social apps as their single most important source of information. In Pew's 2022 edition of the same study, those figures were 43% and 5%.

For sports social teams, this confirms that club and league accounts increasingly act as primary newsrooms rather than secondary channels. Many fans will first hear about signings, injuries, lineups or ticket news in their feeds, not on a website or through traditional media. Official accounts should prioritize speed, accuracy and clear, native-format announcements so they become the trusted first source.

The report also notes that platform algorithms tend to amplify controversial content and that moderation is loosening. Clubs and athletes should plan for rumors spreading quickly by publishing verified updates early, pinning key information and coordinating messaging across platforms. Being consistently reliable is a brand asset that also strengthens value for sponsors attached to official content.

SPORTS MARKETING 9 November 2026 SponCon Sports

Steelers and Snickers turn bye week into 8.7M-view sponsored challenge video

The Pittsburgh Steelers used their bye week, which falls at the midpoint of the NFL season, to publish a sponsored video with Snickers. Players took on a popular social challenge: cutting a Snickers bar into two halves of equal weight. The post tied the idea to splitting the season in half and closed by urging fans to satisfy their halftime hunger. Across channels, the content passed 8.7 million views.

For sports social teams, the lesson is that a familiar trend becomes premium sponsor inventory when timing, wordplay and the product all line up. Calendar moments such as bye weeks, the halfway point of a season or international breaks are often quiet content windows. Building a partner concept around them can fill the gap with something fans want to watch rather than a straightforward advert.

SPORTS MARKETING 5 November 2026 SponCon Sports

MLB postseason lifts social engagement per post by 51%, reshaping sponsorship pricing

An analysis by SponCon Sports and Mondo Metrics of 57,239 posts from all 12 MLB postseason clubs found engagements per post rose 51% and views per post 26% versus the regular season. Even after adjusting for teams shifting more content to Instagram and TikTok, engagement still climbed 32%. Facebook showed the largest jump, with engagements up 79%, while World Series clubs averaged a 168% lift compared with 13% for Wild Card exits.

The takeaway for partnership and social teams is that playoff inventory deserves its own pricing, but not a blanket markup. The author recommends each club calculate its own postseason bump by platform and, ideally, by content series, then refresh those benchmarks every year it qualifies, since algorithms and fan behavior shift. Teams without recent playoff data can use the MLB findings as a starting reference.

SPORTS MARKETING 4 November 2026 Front Office Sports

High school athletic directors now run content operations as NIL and short-form video reshape prep sports

Front Office Sports spoke with around a dozen veteran high school and state athletic administrators across the U.S., who say the job has become far more demanding over the past five years. They link the shift to the arrival of NIL alongside the boom in short-form video. ADs like Ponte Vedra's Mike Harrison now sell videoboard ads, produce hype videos for big games and manage promotion of teams and individual athletes on school social channels.

Families increasingly expect instant, individualized exposure. Sidelines are filling with private content creators hired by athletes, and one Ohio AD said programs without highlight reels posted within an hour of a game are seen as falling behind. To keep up, many schools are recruiting student volunteers interested in tech and journalism to capture and share game content. Actual NIL deals remain rare, but ADs expect compliance work to arrive soon.
