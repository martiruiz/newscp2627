X launches video clipping so users can reshare credited snippets of longer videos

Social News · 10 November 2026 · Source: Social Media Today

X has introduced a clipping option for videos lasting two minutes or more. From the repost menu, users can select a segment between 5 and 60 seconds, capped at 30% of the original, and share it with their own commentary. The clip carries an overlay with the creator's handle, a timestamp showing where it sits in the full video, and a tap-through link to the original.

Both the original poster and the account sharing the clip are credited with resulting impressions, so each can earn through X's Original Creator Rewards program. Creators currently cannot disable clipping; the only way to remove unwanted clips is deleting the original post. Live broadcasts are not yet supported. The feature is live on iOS and Android, with web access to follow.

For sports teams, this rewards publishing longer videos on X, such as press conferences, behind-the-scenes features or highlight packages, since fans and media can clip key moments while driving traffic back to the full upload. Clubs could also clip partner or player content to add commentary. Because clipping can't be switched off, teams should review long uploads carefully before posting.

X 1 November 2026 Social Media Today

X launches invite-only Video Kickstart bonus paying creators for regular vertical video

X has introduced Video Kickstart, an invite-only pilot that pays creators a bonus for posting original short-form vertical videos. To qualify in a roughly month-long period, participants must publish 15 eligible clips on 15 separate days, each under three minutes. Content first shared on other platforms is allowed as long as the creator owns it, and enrollees are fast-tracked into X's ad revenue sharing program.

The move continues X's push into video, following a revamped livestream studio, new overlay options and an updated video composer. For sports social teams, it signals that X will likely reward native vertical video with more visibility, making it worth repurposing owned clips from TikTok, Reels or Shorts rather than only posting links and text updates.

X 28 October 2026 Social Media Today

X begins sharing creator credit when original images are reposted by others

X has updated its Original Content Rewards Program so that credit for reposted images can be shared. According to the report, the change is designed to let more users benefit when engagement builds around linked graphics, rather than rewards flowing only to the account that reposted an image. The short announcement offers few further details on how the credit split will work in practice.

For sports social teams, this matters because so much matchday content on X is visual: lineups, score graphics, player cards and fan-made edits. If original creators gain credit when their images travel, clubs and leagues that produce standout graphics could see more value from wide resharing, and fan artists may be more willing to collaborate.

X 27 October 2026 Front Office Sports

World Wide Wob quits NBA X, citing algorithm shift and weak creator pay

Rob Perez, the NBA personality known as World Wide Wob, has stepped away from his X account of more than one million followers after the Knicks won the title in June. In an interview with Front Office Sports, he pointed to X's move toward a For You recommendation feed, turnover in the platform's sports team, and creator earnings he called uncompetitive versus other platforms, alongside roughly 15 years of nonstop content burnout.

Perez has not disappeared entirely. He streams LA police chases daily on Twitch, posts some NBA content on Threads, where he says he has enjoyed working with the platform's team, and keeps a protected secondary X account for his core community. He compared that approach to YouTubers running alternate channels for content outside their main focus, and did not rule out returning to X someday.
