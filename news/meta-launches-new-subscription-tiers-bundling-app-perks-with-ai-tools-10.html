Facebook

Meta launches new subscription tiers bundling app perks with AI tools for creators

Social News · 18 September 2026 · Source: SocialGeek News

Meta has announced a set of new subscription plans that pair benefits across its main apps with access to artificial intelligence tools. The tiers target businesses, creators and heavy AI users, with monthly prices ranging from $2.99 to $499. The move comes as Meta also pushes its new AI agent, Muse, as part of a broader strategy to diversify revenue and regain investor confidence after a turbulent year.

For sports social teams, this signals that Meta's most capable AI features may increasingly sit behind paid tiers. Clubs, leagues and athletes running large Instagram and Facebook operations should assess whether these plans offer tools that speed up content production, personalise posts for different fan segments or deliver audience insights worth the cost compared with existing workflows.

A practical approach is to trial a lower tier with one account, such as a women's team or academy channel, and measure output time and engagement before scaling. Athletes building personal brands could test AI-assisted content creation, while commercial teams can explore whether the tools help produce more tailored sponsor content across Meta's apps.

FACEBOOK 15 October 2026 SocialGeek News

Facebook launches 'Your Algorithm' letting users tell the feed what they want

Facebook has introduced a feature called "Your Algorithm" that lets people directly shape what appears in their feed. Users can add and manage topics they care about, answer prompts about why they liked or disliked a recommended post, and type in their own words what they want to see more or less of. The system blends these explicit inputs with familiar signals such as likes, comments and watch time.

For sports social teams, this shifts the balance away from pure engagement bait. If a fan tells Facebook they are not interested in a certain type of content, optimising it harder will not help. Topic relevance matters more, so clubs and leagues should make sure their posts are clearly tied to recognisable themes fans would actively choose, like their team, competition, players or specific sport.

SPORTS MARKETING 27 October 2026 Front Office Sports

USC's Jazzy Davidson discusses Venmo NIL campaign and building her personal brand

USC women's basketball standout Jazzy Davidson sat down with Front Office Sports for a video interview covering her partnership with Venmo on its Money Moves campaign and her own habits around managing NIL income. She also talked about her approach to personal branding, being a role model for young fans, playing alongside JuJu Watkins, her pro options across the WNBA, Unrivaled and Project B, and uneven media coverage of women's sports.

For sports social teams, the interview shows how brands are pairing college athletes with practical, values-driven messaging like financial literacy rather than simple product plugs. Athletes who position themselves as role models for kids give sponsors a credible, family-friendly voice, and that kind of story travels well in short video, Q&A and carousel formats.

X 27 October 2026 Front Office Sports

World Wide Wob quits NBA X, citing algorithm shift and weak creator pay

Rob Perez, the NBA personality known as World Wide Wob, has stepped away from his X account of more than one million followers after the Knicks won the title in June. In an interview with Front Office Sports, he pointed to X's move toward a For You recommendation feed, turnover in the platform's sports team, and creator earnings he called uncompetitive versus other platforms, alongside roughly 15 years of nonstop content burnout.

Perez has not disappeared entirely. He streams LA police chases daily on Twitch, posts some NBA content on Threads, where he says he has enjoyed working with the platform's team, and keeps a protected secondary X account for his core community. He compared that approach to YouTubers running alternate channels for content outside their main focus, and did not rule out returning to X someday.
