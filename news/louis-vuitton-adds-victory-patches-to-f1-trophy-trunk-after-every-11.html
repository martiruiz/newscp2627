Louis Vuitton Adds Victory Patches to F1 Trophy Trunk After Every Grand Prix

Social News · 19 October 2026 · Source: SponCon Sports

Louis Vuitton is extending its Formula 1 trophy-trunk partnership with a new ritual: after each Grand Prix, a Victory Patch honouring the winning driver is applied to the trunk. The moment is shared through split-screen videos that pair the patch application with race highlights and podium celebrations, turning the luxury brand's product into part of the story of each win.

For sports social teams, the lesson is to build sponsored content around a repeatable ritual tied to real results. Similar to Red Bull Racing's race-specific stickers with YETI, a recurring format gives fans a reason to follow along all season and gives partners guaranteed, emotionally charged exposure. Clubs could adapt this with trophies, kit details or locker-room items updated after every victory.

The same newsletter offers a related tip from MotoGP's Repsol Lubricants series: give branded formats broad names rather than superlative ones like best move of the race. That flexibility lets content teams publish great moments immediately, while they are still fresh, instead of waiting to crown a single highlight. It also cites research showing sponsored posts outperformed non-sponsored ones at six of 11 F1 teams.

EU draft rules would require influencers to clearly label sponsored content

The European Union's draft Digital Fairness Act seeks to make influencers clearly label sponsored content. The proposal is also expected to include measures to limit compulsive use of online platforms.

Sports organisations that pay athletes, ambassadors or creators to promote partners should review their disclosure practices before the rules take shape. Clubs activating sponsorships through player accounts or creator collaborations will need clear, consistent ad labels in contracts and briefs, which also protects fan trust. Planning compliant formats now avoids last-minute changes to campaigns across EU markets.

SPORTS MARKETING 9 November 2026 SponCon Sports

Steelers and Snickers turn bye week into 8.7M-view sponsored challenge video

The Pittsburgh Steelers used their bye week, which falls at the midpoint of the NFL season, to publish a sponsored video with Snickers. Players took on a popular social challenge: cutting a Snickers bar into two halves of equal weight. The post tied the idea to splitting the season in half and closed by urging fans to satisfy their halftime hunger. Across channels, the content passed 8.7 million views.

For sports social teams, the lesson is that a familiar trend becomes premium sponsor inventory when timing, wordplay and the product all line up. Calendar moments such as bye weeks, the halfway point of a season or international breaks are often quiet content windows. Building a partner concept around them can fill the gap with something fans want to watch rather than a straightforward advert.

SPORTS MARKETING 5 November 2026 SponCon Sports

MLB postseason lifts social engagement per post by 51%, reshaping sponsorship pricing

An analysis by SponCon Sports and Mondo Metrics of 57,239 posts from all 12 MLB postseason clubs found engagements per post rose 51% and views per post 26% versus the regular season. Even after adjusting for teams shifting more content to Instagram and TikTok, engagement still climbed 32%. Facebook showed the largest jump, with engagements up 79%, while World Series clubs averaged a 168% lift compared with 13% for Wild Card exits.

The takeaway for partnership and social teams is that playoff inventory deserves its own pricing, but not a blanket markup. The author recommends each club calculate its own postseason bump by platform and, ideally, by content series, then refresh those benchmarks every year it qualifies, since algorithms and fan behavior shift. Teams without recent playoff data can use the MLB findings as a starting reference.
