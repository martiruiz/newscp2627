Pat McAfee Brings Fan Punt-Catching Contest to ESPN's Monday Night Countdown

Social News · 17 November 2026 · Source: Front Office Sports

Pat McAfee and Jason Kelce staged a "Super Catch" contest on the field before the Ravens-Chargers Monday Night Football game in Baltimore. Army veteran and Ravens fan Richard Nelson, wearing a helmet and pads, caught a roughly 50-yard punt fired from a JUGS machine and won a ticket to Super Bowl LXI, which ESPN will broadcast for the first time on February 14.

It was the second such contest this season, following an October debut in Philadelphia where Kelce caught the punt and lateralled it to an Eagles fan. The bit spins off McAfee's "Kicking is Easy" segment on College GameDay, which has awarded $12 million to students and charities this season. ESPN says the contest will return occasionally rather than weekly.

For sports social teams, this is a reminder that simple, high-stakes fan challenges produce shareable moments with built-in drama, celebrity reactions and an emotional payoff. Clubs can run halftime or pregame skill contests tied to a big prize, film them vertically for TikTok and Reels, and use them to promote marquee events or partners while putting real supporters at the center of the story.

A 60-minute research method for building tailored sponsored content pitches

Sponcon Sports newsletter author Alex Kopilow has outlined a three-step process, collect, compare and customize, for creating brand-specific digital partnership ideas in about an hour. It draws on eight free resources, including secondary social accounts that follow every pro team and league, sports broadcasts as a source of segment names, X search filtered to people you follow, and Meta's Branded Content search and Ad Library.

He also suggests YouTube's Gemini-powered Ask feature to locate sponsor integrations within videos, checking a partner's website, bios and search ads for taglines and priority products, and an AI prompt that finds category terms with double meanings in a given sport, such as linking tire brands to a baseball pitching rotation feature.

SPORTS MARKETING 23 November 2026 SponCon Sports

Dr Pepper reaches college football fans through House of Highlights' CLASH debate series

Dr Pepper has found a way into college football conversations without partnering with a team or league. The brand worked with CLASH, the debate and trivia channel from House of Highlights, on three episodes featuring coach Ed Orgeron, creator MMG and former Heisman winner Troy Smith. Alongside product placement and branded messaging, the activation added merchandise QR codes and paid ads running against the videos, pushing views past 2 million.

The lesson for sports marketers is that fan attention increasingly sits with media brands and creators, not only official rights holders. Brands can build relevance in a sport by sponsoring the shows and debate formats fans already watch, then layering commerce hooks such as QR codes and paid amplification to extend reach and measure response.

Tobin Heath and Christen Press Land Multi-Year Ally Deal, Discuss Athlete-Led Media

Former USWNT stars Tobin Heath and Christen Press sat down with Front Office Sports for a video interview. They covered their new multi-year, multimillion-dollar partnership with Ally, how far the NWSL has come since its first championship, and their work building athlete-led media and businesses through their company Re-Inc. They also pointed to infrastructure and independent ownership as the next big growth opportunities for women's soccer.

For sports social teams, the deal shows that sponsors will commit long-term money to athletes who own their media channels and audiences, not only to leagues and clubs. Women's soccer clubs and the NWSL can treat athlete-run brands like Re-Inc as distribution partners, co-creating content that reaches fans the official accounts may not.
