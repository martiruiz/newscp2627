Manchester United and Snapdragon turn Champions League Starting XI graphics into illustrated portraits

Social News · 26 October 2026 · Source: SponCon Sports

Manchester United has commissioned Stanley Chow, a local illustrator and lifelong supporter, to draw custom portraits of the club's Starting XI for its Champions League fixtures this season. Each player appears in the Snapdragon-branded kit, and the artwork is photographed on Snapdragon-powered phones against scenic backdrops in host cities such as Como and Madrid.

On Instagram, United is pairing the carousel with the platform's new multiple captions feature, labelling each player as fans swipe through the portraits. The approach gives a routine matchday asset a premium, collectible feel while weaving the sponsor's product into the story rather than simply stamping a logo on a template.

For social teams, the takeaway is that lineup announcements are among the most reliable sponsorship inventory and can be refreshed creatively. Clubs could commission fan artists for marquee fixtures, tie visuals to away-trip locations, or let a tech partner's device become part of the production. Testing Instagram's per-slide captions on carousels is an easy way to add context without cluttering the artwork.

NFL, Commanders and ESPN use Instagram collabs to hype Sam Hartman's Bachelor casting

Washington Commanders practice squad quarterback Sam Hartman was announced as the lead for the 30th season of ABC's The Bachelor. Right after the reveal on Good Morning America, the NFL, the Commanders and ESPN published Instagram collaboration posts with ABC and the show's official account, plus similar TikTok content. The NFL's slow-motion clip of Hartman's Combine 40-yard dash passed a million views by midday, while static photos from the team and ESPN drew over 400,000 combined likes.

The push fits a wider NFL strategy: the league previously said it is scaling up female-focused content, including training camp videos featuring quarterbacks with their partners. ESPN also plans to keep coordinating with Disney sister brands on Bachelor content relevant to its fans. Hartman's former college programs, Wake Forest and Notre Dame, joined in as well.

Meta's Edits app adds speed curves, auto audio balancing and text timing controls

Meta has rolled out a new batch of features for Edits, its video editing app tied to Instagram. Creators can now apply speed curves to vary playback speed within a clip, choosing from presets or building their own. An auto-adjust tool balances audio levels across video elements, and new controls let users set how long text animations stay on screen and how fast they loop.

Edits also now lets users pick which topics they want to see more or less of in its Inspiration tab, similar to Meta's Your Algorithm tool for feeds. The update continues Meta's near-biweekly release cadence for the app, which has also recently arrived on desktop, giving editors a larger workspace.

INSTAGRAM 12 November 2026 Social Media Today

Instagram tests feature letting users pick favorite sports and leagues for updates

Instagram appears to be testing a sports preferences option, spotted by app researcher Alessandro Paluzzi. Users would choose from a list of major sports and tailor which league updates they receive. Instagram would then use those choices to surface real-time sports updates and show more sports content to interested users. The move follows Threads adding sports-focused elements to capture live event discussion. Instagram has not commented on the test.

For clubs and leagues, this could mean that fans who declare an interest in a sport or league are more likely to be shown related posts, including live moments. If the feature launches, teams should lean into timely, real-time content around matches, such as quick results graphics, goal clips and reaction Reels, so they are well placed to benefit from any boost in sports-focused distribution.
