Mosseri explains Instagram ranking signals: engagement rate, timing, originality and testing

Social News · 1 October 2026 · Source: SocialGeek News

Instagram head Adam Mosseri has released new videos breaking down how the platform decides what to show users. His key points: interaction rates matter more than raw views or likes, posting time still counts because the feed stays roughly chronological, original content is prioritised over reposts, and small accounts can be shown to non-followers to test their reach. He also noted that AI models increasingly drive ranking.

For club and league social teams, the message is to judge posts by engagement rate rather than view counts, and to schedule content around when fans are actually online, such as kick-off, half-time and full-time windows. Reposting broadcaster clips or fan videos is likely to be outranked by the original creator, so in-house footage from training, the tunnel and the dressing room should carry more weight.

Mosseri also stressed constant experimentation, since what works one week may not work the next. Trial Reels let teams test formats with non-followers without risking their main feed, which suits matchday experiments, player challenges or new series. Instagram has also published a 2026 Reels creation guide covering editing, music, captions, Templates, Remix and Collab, offering a useful reference for refreshing workflows.

Manchester United and Snapdragon turn Champions League Starting XI graphics into illustrated portraits

Manchester United has commissioned Stanley Chow, a local illustrator and lifelong supporter, to draw custom portraits of the club's Starting XI for its Champions League fixtures this season. Each player appears in the Snapdragon-branded kit, and the artwork is photographed on Snapdragon-powered phones against scenic backdrops in host cities such as Como and Madrid.

On Instagram, United is pairing the carousel with the platform's new multiple captions feature, labelling each player as fans swipe through the portraits. The approach gives a routine matchday asset a premium, collectible feel while weaving the sponsor's product into the story rather than simply stamping a logo on a template.

Meta's Edits app launches iPhone Duo version and tests desktop editor

Meta has released a dedicated version of Edits, its short-form video editing app, built for Apple's folding iPhone Duo. The interface adapts to the device's larger screen, orientation and crease, letting users edit and preview their timeline side by side. Separately, a desktop version first announced in June is now in live testing with selected creators, according to a screenshot shared by creator Taylor Golub.

The desktop layout shows more editing elements on one screen, making it easier to move between the app's creation tools. INSTAGRAM 8 October 2026 SocialGeek News

Instagram tests Reels-first home screen and a paid read-only browsing mode

Instagram is testing a redesigned home in which Reels fill the first tab when the app opens, with Stories sitting just above them. The familiar feed of followed accounts, including a chronological option, sits one swipe away, and sound plays by default. Users can switch back to the old layout in Settings. Separately, Instagram is trialling a read-only mode that blocks likes, comments, DMs, follows and Story views, reportedly tied to its paid Instagram Plus package.

For sports social teams, the Reels-first home is another signal that Meta is prioritising short video and recommended content over posts from followed accounts. Clubs that still lean on static graphics such as lineups, final scores or sponsor cards should plan vertical video versions built for discovery, with strong opening seconds and audio designed to be heard, since sound now plays automatically.
