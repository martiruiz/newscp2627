Aaron Rodgers' McAfee Show interview shows the power of athlete-friendly platforms

Social News · 25 November 2026 · Source: Front Office Sports

Aaron Rodgers used The Pat McAfee Show to respond to being benched by the Pittsburgh Steelers, delivering a pointed critique of head coach Mike McCarthy. With ESPN and ESPN2 busy with college basketball, the TV broadcast moved to ESPNews, but the show's YouTube stream had passed 622,000 viewers by press time. The Steelers released Rodgers shortly after the interview.

Front Office Sports frames McAfee as the big winner. His long relationship with Rodgers, and a relaxed format that lets stars talk without pushback, keeps landing interviews that dominate the sports conversation. The piece links that pull to ESPN's reported extension talks with McAfee. It also notes LeBron James chose the same show to answer criticism of his son Bronny.

For sports social teams, the lesson is that athletes increasingly go to trusted, personality-led shows and creator-style streams to control their story. That can leave clubs reacting in real time. Teams should track these channels closely, have response protocols ready, and consider building their own comfortable, long-form formats where players want to speak first, instead of turning to outside hosts.

Dan Rapaport discusses the Internet Invitational and where YouTube golf goes next

Scratch Golf's Dan Rapaport joined Front Office Sports' David Rumsey for a video conversation on golf's evolving media landscape. They covered his role calling the Skins Game on Prime Video on Black Friday, why players have to carry a made-for-TV format, whether Tiger Woods is now a special-event golfer, and the future of the Internet Invitational and the broader YouTube golf scene.

For sports social teams, the discussion shows how creator-led competitions on YouTube now sit alongside streaming-platform specials as serious golf properties. Clubs, tours and athletes can borrow the formula: personality-driven, made-for-digital events where talent carries the entertainment. Partnering with established golf creators or building collaborative YouTube formats can reach younger fans who follow the sport through creators rather than traditional broadcasts.

YOUTUBE 24 November 2026 Front Office Sports

Kalshi sponsors 49ers WAGs' YouTube series to reach female NFL fans

Prediction market Kalshi has partnered with Wife'd Up, Mic'd Up, the YouTube series hosted by 49ers spouses Kristin Juszczyk and Claire Kittle. The six-episode deal features a guest partner of another NFL player in each installment, plus a branded prediction segment tied to her gameday. The first episode, with Haley Cavinder, passed 130,000 views, and launch videos on Instagram drew nearly 700,000 views combined.

The integration is built natively into creator formats: a get-ready-with-me announcement, onscreen QR codes, sign-up links in descriptions and segments woven into real travel and gameday stories. Kalshi says the goal is reaching new audiences, mainly women, whose presence on the platform it says grew about 60% in a year. The NFL bans players from endorsing prediction markets, raising questions since Kyle Juszczyk appears in the content.

YouTube public view counts now estimate people watching together on TV

YouTube's public counters now include an estimate of people watching a video together on a TV screen, covering long-form videos, Shorts and live streams. Previously accumulated totals are not being recalculated, and creator payouts are unchanged by the update.

Sports content is often watched in groups, from highlights on the living-room TV to live shows and watch-alongs, so clubs and leagues may see visible view numbers rise on connected-TV content. Social teams should note the change date when comparing performance over time and when reporting reach to sponsors, since older figures will not be adjusted.
