Instagram tests feature letting users pick favorite sports and leagues for updates

Social News · 12 November 2026 · Source: Social Media Today

Instagram appears to be testing a sports preferences option, spotted by app researcher Alessandro Paluzzi. Users would choose from a list of major sports and tailor which league updates they receive. Instagram would then use those choices to surface real-time sports updates and show more sports content to interested users. The move follows Threads adding sports-focused elements to capture live event discussion. Instagram has not commented on the test.

For clubs and leagues, this could mean that fans who declare an interest in a sport or league are more likely to be shown related posts, including live moments. If the feature launches, teams should lean into timely, real-time content around matches, such as quick results graphics, goal clips and reaction Reels, so they are well placed to benefit from any boost in sports-focused distribution.

Meta has repeatedly highlighted sports as a major engagement driver across its apps, including growing interest in women's sport, with the top 10 women athletes drawing 128 million combined followers on Instagram. Women's teams and athletes in particular should watch this test closely and consider how consistent, fast-turnaround match-day posting could help them reach new, opted-in audiences.

Instagram lets creators tag Amazon affiliate products directly in Reels and feed posts

Instagram now allows creators to tag Amazon products with affiliate links inside their content, up to 30 products per Reel and 20 per feed post. Once a creator connects an Amazon account through the Professional Dashboard, affiliate attribution is added automatically. Instagram takes no cut; Amazon continues paying creators for qualifying purchases.

For sports, this cuts the friction of the old 'link in bio' approach. Athletes can tag the boots, recovery gear or training equipment they actually use in a workout Reel, and fans can buy without leaving the video. Recommendation content becomes a direct revenue line for athletes and creators.

INSTAGRAM 16 November 2026 Social Media Today

Instagram launches dedicated app for Apple's foldable iPhone Duo with side-by-side Reels

Instagram has released a version of its app built specifically for the iPhone Duo, using the device's larger unfolded display. The main feed opens in a wider format, Reels show the video and its comments next to each other while playback continues, control buttons move to a sidebar, and messages open in a two-column layout. When the phone is folded, Stories and Reels keep their usual vertical format.

For sports social teams, the side-by-side Reels view puts the comment section on equal footing with the clip itself. That makes it worth seeding conversation early: pinned replies from the club account, prompts asking fans to predict a result or rate a goal, and quick responses to top comments can all become part of the viewing experience rather than an afterthought.

INSTAGRAM 13 November 2026 Front Office Sports

NFL, Commanders and ESPN use Instagram collabs to hype Sam Hartman's Bachelor casting

Washington Commanders practice squad quarterback Sam Hartman was announced as the lead for the 30th season of ABC's The Bachelor. Right after the reveal on Good Morning America, the NFL, the Commanders and ESPN published Instagram collaboration posts with ABC and the show's official account, plus similar TikTok content. The NFL's slow-motion clip of Hartman's Combine 40-yard dash passed a million views by midday, while static photos from the team and ESPN drew over 400,000 combined likes.

The push fits a wider NFL strategy: the league previously said it is scaling up female-focused content, including training camp videos featuring quarterbacks with their partners. ESPN also plans to keep coordinating with Disney sister brands on Bachelor content relevant to its fans. Hartman's former college programs, Wake Forest and Notre Dame, joined in as well.
