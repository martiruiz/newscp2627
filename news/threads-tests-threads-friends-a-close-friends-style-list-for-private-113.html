Threads tests Threads Friends, a Close Friends-style list for private posting

Social News · 29 October 2026 · Source: Social Media Today

Threads is trialling Threads Friends, a feature that lets users build a private list of up to 50 connections and publish posts visible only to that group. It works much like Instagram's Close Friends: people added can see they are on the list, and members can view each other's likes and replies on those posts. The test is running in Ireland, Italy and Japan, though anyone worldwide can be added to a list.

For sports social teams, this points to a broader shift from public broadcasting toward smaller, curated sharing spaces. Athletes could use a Friends list to share behind-the-scenes thoughts with family, teammates or a handful of trusted creators, while clubs might reserve it for media partners, ambassadors or a select group of superfans.

With a 50-person cap, the feature suits VIP-style activations rather than mass reach. Clubs could reward contest winners or season-ticket holders with temporary access to exclusive updates, and teams already running Instagram Close Friends programmes should watch whether Threads rolls the option out more widely in their markets.

NBA expands creator partnership with Kenny Beecham and Enjoy Basketball

The NBA has widened its content deal with creator Kenny Beecham and his Enjoy Basketball brand. A three-episode pilot of Enjoy the NBA Trivia Show becomes an eight-episode season on NBA TV, the NBA app and Enjoy Basketball's YouTube channel. Other shows from the brand will run on league platforms, and the creators also get access to official NBA footage.

He says the group will keep criticising the league when it disagrees, and that the NBA values that authenticity.

YOUTUBE 12 November 2026 SponCon Sports

Mercedes F1's Antonelli title interview shows how YouTube packaging makes or breaks sponsored content

After Kimi Antonelli became Formula 1's youngest World Champion, Mercedes released an exclusive interview on its Nu-sponsored video podcast. Despite a channel of over 2M subscribers, the 30-minute episode drew about 30K views and nine comments in six days. Its title led with the series name and episode number, and the thumbnail was crowded with show branding and Nu's logo. F1's own interview, framed clearly as the champion's first interview, reached 935K views in a week.

YouTube strategist Gen Kimura argued Mercedes did not lead with value: YouTube is intent-driven, so viewers need a reason to click. He also called subscriber counts a vanity metric, saying brands increasingly look at a channel's consistent view floor. Meanwhile, Mercedes' Instagram announcement and clips generated 2.9M views and over 727K engagements, a reminder to measure sponsor value across the whole content package.

INSTAGRAM 12 November 2026 SocialGeek News

Instagram tests Features, horizontal videos up to 90 minutes, to rival YouTube

Instagram is testing a format called Features that lets creators upload horizontal videos of up to 90 minutes, aimed at podcasts, interviews and entertainment shows. Viewers will be able to jump from a short Reel straight to the full video without leaving the app. The test is running with selected creators in the US after earlier trials in Brazil, with a separate upload flow and snippet clips published as Reels.

For clubs, leagues and athletes, this opens a path to host long-form content where much of their audience already lives. Full press conferences, behind-the-scenes documentaries, club podcasts or extended match analysis could sit on Instagram, with short Reels acting as trailers that drive fans to the complete video.
