Threads tests Threads Friends, a Close Friends-style list for private posting

Social News · 29 October 2026 · Source: Social Media Today

Threads is trialling Threads Friends, a feature that lets users build a private list of up to 50 connections and publish posts visible only to that group. It works much like Instagram's Close Friends: people added can see they are on the list, and members can view each other's likes and replies on those posts. The test is running in Ireland, Italy and Japan, though anyone worldwide can be added to a list.

For sports social teams, this points to a broader shift from public broadcasting toward smaller, curated sharing spaces. Athletes could use a Friends list to share behind-the-scenes thoughts with family, teammates or a handful of trusted creators, while clubs might reserve it for media partners, ambassadors or a select group of superfans.

With a 50-person cap, the feature suits VIP-style activations rather than mass reach. Clubs could reward contest winners or season-ticket holders with temporary access to exclusive updates, and teams already running Instagram Close Friends programmes should watch whether Threads rolls the option out more widely in their markets.

THREADS 23 November 2026 Social Media Today

Threads previews dedicated iPad app with larger feed layout, launch date unconfirmed

Threads has previewed a dedicated iPad version of its app. Head of Design Christopher Clare shared screenshots showing the feed stretched across a tablet screen, giving users more room to scroll and read posts. He said the app is coming very soon but gave no release date. iPad users currently rely on the browser or the scaled-up iPhone app.

The move follows Meta's launch of a dedicated iPad version of Instagram last year after long-running user demand, and continued refinements to the Threads desktop experience. Meta appears to be treating tablet support as a higher priority this time, bringing Threads to iPad much faster than its other apps.

THREADS 19 November 2026 Social Media Today

Threads tests upvoted Q&A sessions inside live chats

Threads is trialling a Q&A format within its live chats. Hosts can collect questions from participants, and the chat then votes on which ones should be asked, creating a more structured, interview-style session. The approach resembles Reddit's AMA model, where the community steers the conversation by upvoting. The test is running in a limited number of live chat sessions, though every participant can read the answers.

The feature builds on live chat, which Threads launched in April and expanded in September, as Meta pushes the app toward real-time conversation around major events. X still leads for live event discussion, but Threads is gaining ground and wants to be a stronger option for communities and brands looking to engage audiences in the moment.

YOUTUBE 24 November 2026 Front Office Sports

Dan Rapaport discusses the Internet Invitational and where YouTube golf goes next

Scratch Golf's Dan Rapaport joined Front Office Sports' David Rumsey for a video conversation on golf's evolving media landscape. They covered his role calling the Skins Game on Prime Video on Black Friday, why players have to carry a made-for-TV format, whether Tiger Woods is now a special-event golfer, and the future of the Internet Invitational and the broader YouTube golf scene.

For sports social teams, the discussion shows how creator-led competitions on YouTube now sit alongside streaming-platform specials as serious golf properties. Clubs, tours and athletes can borrow the formula: personality-driven, made-for-digital events where talent carries the entertainment. Partnering with established golf creators or building collaborative YouTube formats can reach younger fans who follow the sport through creators rather than traditional broadcasts.
