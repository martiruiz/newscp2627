LinkedIn

LinkedIn tests simplified navigation and longer text previews in the feed

Social News · 27 October 2026 · Source: Social Media Today

LinkedIn is experimenting with a streamlined navigation layout on desktop and mobile. On desktop, menus are being merged into fewer tabs: the Profile tab now gathers analytics, saved posts and settings, while a new More tab houses puzzle games, news, groups, events, newsletters and video. Mobile users will see similar consolidation in the bottom bar. The test is rolling out gradually and varies by region and language.

LinkedIn is also displaying more of each text post in the main feed before readers need to tap the more link. That could lift engagement for creators, though it weakens the old tactic of hiding the main message behind the cut-off with line-by-line posts.

For clubs, leagues and sports brands using LinkedIn for sponsorship, partnership and business storytelling, the takeaway is to front-load value: open with the key result, partner name or insight, since readers will now see more before clicking. With newsletters, events and video moving under a More tab, teams should actively promote those formats in posts so they remain visible, and keep an eye on analytics now sitting under the Profile tab.

X 28 October 2026 Social Media Today

X begins sharing creator credit when original images are reposted by others

X has updated its Original Content Rewards Program so that credit for reposted images can be shared. According to the report, the change is designed to let more users benefit when engagement builds around linked graphics, rather than rewards flowing only to the account that reposted an image. The short announcement offers few further details on how the credit split will work in practice.

For sports social teams, this matters because so much matchday content on X is visual: lineups, score graphics, player cards and fan-made edits. If original creators gain credit when their images travel, clubs and leagues that produce standout graphics could see more value from wide resharing, and fan artists may be more willing to collaborate.

SPORTS MARKETING 28 October 2026 Front Office Sports

Women's sports bars push back on WNBA's 12:30 p.m. Finals Game 6 tip-off

The WNBA moved Game 6 of its Finals from 8 p.m. to 12:30 p.m. ET on a Thursday so the Atlanta Dream could play at the 17,000-seat State Farm Arena instead of the 3,500-seat Gateway Center. With Golden State up 3-2, a potential title clincher now starts at 9:30 a.m. in San Francisco. Owners of women's sports bars in Chicago, Brooklyn, Portland and Seattle told Front Office Sports the slot hurts fans and their revenue.

Several owners voiced frustration publicly, starting with an Instagram post from Level Sporting Club's founder. The Sports Bra's founder warned that weaker viewing numbers could be misread as proof the audience is missing. Seattle's Rough & Tumble said it may not open, risking roughly $10,000 to $15,000 per Finals night. Level Sporting Club chose to open anyway, promoting the game on social as a remote-work gathering, a first weekday morning opening for the bar.

INSTAGRAM 27 October 2026 Social Media Today

Meta's Edits app adds Halloween effects, beat-synced animations and bulk caption editing

Meta has rolled out a new batch of updates to Edits, its video editing app tied to Instagram. The release includes Halloween-themed fonts, sound effects and animations, 13 new text animations, and beat-synced effects such as flashing lights and zooms that automatically match a clip's music. Caption tools gain extra styles and bulk editing, while new automated color correction and per-clip volume boosts up to 150% round out the update.

Edits' carousel editor, launched on iOS earlier this month, is now also available on Android, and a new filter was created with Nigerian musician Arya Starr. Meta says a desktop version is coming soon, which would move the app closer to being a full production studio rather than a quick mobile editor.
