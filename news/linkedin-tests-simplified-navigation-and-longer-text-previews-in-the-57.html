LinkedIn

LinkedIn tests simplified navigation and longer text previews in the feed

Social News · 27 October 2026 · Source: Social Media Today

LinkedIn is experimenting with a streamlined navigation layout on desktop and mobile. On desktop, menus are being merged into fewer tabs: the Profile tab now gathers analytics, saved posts and settings, while a new More tab houses puzzle games, news, groups, events, newsletters and video. Mobile users will see similar consolidation in the bottom bar. The test is rolling out gradually and varies by region and language.

LinkedIn is also displaying more of each text post in the main feed before readers need to tap the more link. That could lift engagement for creators, though it weakens the old tactic of hiding the main message behind the cut-off with line-by-line posts.

For clubs, leagues and sports brands using LinkedIn for sponsorship, partnership and business storytelling, the takeaway is to front-load value: open with the key result, partner name or insight, since readers will now see more before clicking. With newsletters, events and video moving under a More tab, teams should actively promote those formats in posts so they remain visible, and keep an eye on analytics now sitting under the Profile tab.

SPORTS MARKETING 2 November 2026 SponCon Sports

Internet Invitational Shows How Creator Golf Turns Sponsors Into Part of the Show

The second Internet Invitational, the creator golf event from Barstool Sports and Bob Does Sports, has built its sponsorship model around brands that join the entertainment instead of interrupting it. Dunkin' returns as presenting sponsor with an on-course drive-thru, Zyrtec owns a branded Relief Hole, and Reese's backs a $500K hole-in-one challenge. With $4M at stake, pressure is part of the appeal.

WHOOP also brought back its WHOOP Live activation, first used at the Ryder Cup, which shows players' heart rates during tense moments. That turns biometric data into a storytelling tool that heightens drama for viewers while keeping the partner visibly tied to the action rather than placed in a separate ad break.

X 1 November 2026 Social Media Today

X launches invite-only Video Kickstart bonus paying creators for regular vertical video

X has introduced Video Kickstart, an invite-only pilot that pays creators a bonus for posting original short-form vertical videos. To qualify in a roughly month-long period, participants must publish 15 eligible clips on 15 separate days, each under three minutes. Content first shared on other platforms is allowed as long as the creator owns it, and enrollees are fast-tracked into X's ad revenue sharing program.

The move continues X's push into video, following a revamped livestream studio, new overlay options and an updated video composer. For sports social teams, it signals that X will likely reward native vertical video with more visibility, making it worth repurposing owned clips from TikTok, Reels or Shorts rather than only posting links and text updates.

THREADS 29 October 2026 Social Media Today

Threads tests Threads Friends, a Close Friends-style list for private posting

Threads is trialling Threads Friends, a feature that lets users build a private list of up to 50 connections and publish posts visible only to that group. It works much like Instagram's Close Friends: people added can see they are on the list, and members can view each other's likes and replies on those posts. The test is running in Ireland, Italy and Japan, though anyone worldwide can be added to a list.

For sports social teams, this points to a broader shift from public broadcasting toward smaller, curated sharing spaces. Athletes could use a Friends list to share behind-the-scenes thoughts with family, teammates or a handful of trusted creators, while clubs might reserve it for media partners, ambassadors or a select group of superfans.
