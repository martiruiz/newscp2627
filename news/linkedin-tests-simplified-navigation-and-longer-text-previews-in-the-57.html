LinkedIn tests simplified navigation and longer text previews in the feed

Social News · 27 October 2026 · Source: Social Media Today

LinkedIn is experimenting with a streamlined navigation layout on desktop and mobile. On desktop, menus are being merged into fewer tabs: the Profile tab now gathers analytics, saved posts and settings, while a new More tab houses puzzle games, news, groups, events, newsletters and video. Mobile users will see similar consolidation in the bottom bar. The test is rolling out gradually and varies by region and language.

LinkedIn is also displaying more of each text post in the main feed before readers need to tap the more link. That could lift engagement for creators, though it weakens the old tactic of hiding the main message behind the cut-off with line-by-line posts.

For clubs, leagues and sports brands using LinkedIn for sponsorship, partnership and business storytelling, the takeaway is to front-load value: open with the key result, partner name or insight, since readers will now see more before clicking. With newsletters, events and video moving under a More tab, teams should actively promote those formats in posts so they remain visible, and keep an eye on analytics now sitting under the Profile tab.

LinkedIn tests showing how often each person revisits your profile

LinkedIn is trialling a frequent viewer indicator for Premium subscribers, according to a Wired report. Beyond the existing list of who viewed a profile, the feature would show how many times a specific person returned to that page within a set period. LinkedIn confirmed to Wired that the feature is in testing but shared no further details, including timing or wider availability.

Some marketers already treat profile viewers as a source of warm leads, and a repeat-visit count could signal stronger interest. There are privacy concerns too: frequent visits may reflect non-professional curiosity, though users can restrict profile visibility to connections only and have no obligation to reach out to anyone who appears on the list.

LINKEDIN 9 November 2026 Social Media Today

LinkedIn details AI Profile Relevance model shaping which non-connection posts users see

LinkedIn has explained a new Profile Relevance model that decides which posts from people outside a member's network appear in their feed. The system uses LLM-generated summaries of each member's profile and posting history, then scores posts against factors such as topic, location, job title, company, industry, seniority and education. In tests, LinkedIn reported a 0.54% lift in feed updates viewed, a 0.94% rise in viral rate and an 18.8% drop in hide rate.

For sports organisations, this means LinkedIn reach beyond followers increasingly depends on how clearly a post signals its professional context. Clubs and leagues using the platform for sponsorship, partnerships, hiring or business storytelling should be explicit about topic, industry and location, so the model can match content to sponsors, agencies, media buyers and sports-business professionals who don't yet follow them.

LINKEDIN 3 November 2026 Social Media Today

LinkedIn adds post performance table and new saves, sends and network metrics

LinkedIn has explained how its refreshed creator analytics will work, following last month's broader creator tools update. A new Post Performance Table lists every post alongside its impressions, views and watch time, so users can compare content without opening each post's analytics separately. LinkedIn's content product director Sam Corrao Clannon outlined the changes, adding that the company will watch how creators use the data before deciding on further improvements.

Several metrics have also been expanded. Creators can now see which specific members followed them from a given post, how many people saved or sent a post to someone else, and how many views came from connections versus people outside their network. LinkedIn says it is also working on new monetization options for creators as it pushes for more feed engagement.
