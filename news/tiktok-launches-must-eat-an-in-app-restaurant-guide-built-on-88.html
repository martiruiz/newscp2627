TikTok launches Must Eat, an in-app restaurant guide built on community recommendations

Social News · 28 October 2026 · Source: Social Media Today

TikTok has rolled out Must Eat, a restaurant guide inside the app covering several U.S. regions. Users can browse by city and filter by cuisine, price, score and location. Each restaurant page shows neighborhood details, price ranges and related TikTok videos. Some venues can be booked directly through OpenTable, and spots can be saved to existing TikTok collections. The feature extends the local feed TikTok introduced in February.

TikTok says 27 million Americans visited an independent restaurant, cafe or shop for the first time last year after finding it on the platform. The company also points to Douyin, its Chinese counterpart, which moved from a restaurant guide into in-app food ordering, suggesting TikTok may push further into local commerce.

For sports teams, this signals that location-based, food-focused content is getting more visibility on TikTok. Clubs can create matchday food content featuring stadium concessions, hospitality areas and nearby bars or restaurant partners, helping fans plan game days. Sponsorship teams can pitch local food and drink partners co-branded videos tied to home fixtures, while athletes can share favorite spots in their city to build authentic local connections.

TikTok US removes over 14 million fake accounts in second-quarter enforcement report

TikTok has released account enforcement data for its US operation covering the second quarter. The platform says it removed more than 14 million fake accounts during the period. It also says it blocked close to 65 million spam accounts before they could be created, stopping them at the sign-up stage.

For sports social teams, large fake-account purges can cause sudden dips in follower counts and engagement figures. Clubs, leagues and athletes should expect occasional drops and avoid treating them as a loss of real fans. Benchmarking against engaged metrics such as watch time, shares and comments gives a more reliable picture than raw follower totals.

TIKTOK 25 October 2026 Social Media Today

TikTok US removes 14.6 million fake accounts and 781 million fake followers in Q2

TikTok USDS, the US-based group running the app's American operations since January, released its second enforcement report, covering April to June. It removed 14.6 million fake accounts, blocked 64.8 million spam accounts at sign-up, stripped 781.4 million fake followers and stopped 403.2 million fake follow requests, a clear jump on the first quarter. Video removals and livestream warnings or demonetizations also rose.

Most video takedowns fell under regulated goods and services, a broad category covering gambling, alcohol, counterfeit products and services selling fake likes, views and followers. TikTok also said it disrupted 30 covert networks trying to manipulate engagement, removing more than 76,000 accounts and 4.8 million posts, as it ramps up integrity efforts ahead of the US midterms.

TikTok lets users repost individual comments, turning replies into standalone content

TikTok is rolling out a feature that allows people to repost a single comment without sharing the original video. Funny, useful or interesting comments can now earn reach on their own, and replies can also be discovered outside the thread where they first appeared. The comment section effectively becomes a content format in its own right rather than a secondary space beneath the video.

For sports social teams, this makes community management a direct growth lever. A sharp reply from the club account, a witty reaction from a player, or a standout fan comment after a big result can now travel independently and reach audiences who never saw the original clip. That raises the value of having fast, on-brand responders ready during matches and key moments.
